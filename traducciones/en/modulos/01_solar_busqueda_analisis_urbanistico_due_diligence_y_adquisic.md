# Plot: search, planning analysis, due diligence and acquisition

> **Objective:** buy a plot that can be built on, at the price the project can bear and with no hidden encumbrances.
> **Duration:** 120 min · **Result:** plot conveyed by deed to the SPE (project company) or an option to buy signed.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| TRLSRU (RDL 7/2015), art. 21 | The basic status of land: rural or urbanised. Only urbanised land with services can be built on directly |
| Regional planning law | Defines "solar" (building plot), land classification, land transfers and charges. See Annex B |
| PGOU + municipal by-laws | Buildability, heights, uses, setbacks, required parking spaces |
| Ley 12/2023 (housing), DF 4.ª | In new urbanisations: a minimum land reserve for protected housing (40 % on rural land being urbanised, 20 % in renewal schemes on urbanised land), unless regional rules say otherwise |
| Mortgage Act and Mortgage Regulation (Ley Hipotecaria y Reglamento Hipotecario) | What the Land Registry says is what counts against third parties |
| RDL 1/1993 (ITP-AJD) and Ley 37/1992 (IVA) | How the purchase is taxed |
| RDL 1/2010 (LSC) + Ley 18/2022 | Incorporating the SPE. Minimum capital of an SL: 1 € (with protection rules until it reaches 3.000 €) |
| Ley 10/2010 (money laundering) | Identifying the seller and the origin of the funds |

## Lesson 1.1 — Define the product and find the plot

- **Before you search, decide what you are going to develop.** It is the filter you will look at every plot through.
  - **Type of development:** open-market housing, protected housing (your autonomous community's scheme), incentivised affordable housing (Ley 12/2023) or mixed. It changes the maximum price, the IVA (4 % on protected housing under the special scheme), the financing and the buyer.
  - **Target buyers:** first home, trading up, investor, foreign buyer or second home.
  - **Approximate number of homes, unit types and floor areas.**
  - **Specification level**, garages, storerooms and common areas (pool, garden, gym, coworking).
  - **Target sale price** (€/m²) and **maximum land budget** (residual method, Module 2).
  - Result: a one-page **product sheet**.
- **Analyse the location** of each plot: transport links, public transport, services, facilities (schools, healthcare, shops) and housing demand in the area.

- **What you are looking for:** consolidated urban land, with services (road access, water, sewerage, electricity) and with set building lines. It is the only land on which you can apply for a licence without urbanising first.
- **Unconsolidated urban or developable land:** cheaper, but it needs reparcelling, land transfers and urbanisation works. Years of lead time. Not for your first development.
- **Channels, from most to least profitable:**
  1. Your own network: notaries (inheritances), lawyers, property managers, land agents, owners of old houses in areas with unused buildability.
  2. Active search in the PGOU: find blocks with unused buildability and write to the owners (address from the Cadastre).
  3. Portals: Idealista and Fotocasa (land section), Solvia, Servihabitat, Altamira, Haya, Aliseda.
  4. SAREB and bank servicers.
  5. Auctions: BOE, AEAT, Social Security, town halls.
  6. Public land: CASA47 (the state body that has replaced SEPES since 2025) and your community's land agency (Annex A).
  7. Investment funds and financial institutions selling land portfolios.
  8. Off-market deals: agreements with owners through a swap for future homes or by contributing the land to the company.
- **10-minute filter before you spend more time:**
  - Buildability × sale price in the area ÷ asking price → if the land exceeds 30 % of revenue, drop it unless the location is prime.
  - Is it open-market housing, or is there a protected-housing reserve?
  - Does anything need demolishing, or anyone need moving out?
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** falling in love with the location without running the numbers; buying "developable" land thinking it is buildable; not checking whether the town hall is changing the plan.

## Lesson 1.2 — Planning analysis: what you can build

- **Who decides:** the autonomous community makes the planning law, and the town hall applies its PGOU and by-laws.
- **Documents you must read:**
  - **PGOU / PGM:** classification (urban, developable, rural) and zoning (residential, commercial, public facilities).
  - **Zone sheet or by-law:** the plot's parameters.
  - **Partial plan, special plan or detailed study** if the plot is inside a planning area.
  - **Heritage catalogue:** if the existing building is protected, you cannot demolish it.
- **Parameters you have to work out:**
  - **Buildability (m²t/m²s):** m² of floor area per m² of land. Example: 1.000 m² × 2 m²t/m²s = 2.000 m² of floor area.
  - **Maximum height:** in storeys (PB+4: ground floor plus four) and in metres.
  - **Maximum site coverage** at ground level (%).
  - **Setbacks** from the street front, boundaries and rear.
  - **Permitted** and compatible **uses**.
  - **Parking spaces** required (usually 1 per home or per 100 m²; it depends on the by-law).
  - **Maximum density** of homes per hectare, if there is one. It caps the number of flats.
  - **What counts** towards buildability (terraces, basements, penthouses). It varies a lot between municipalities.
- **How to do it in practice:**
  1. Download the PGOU from the municipal viewer or the regional planning viewer.
  2. Ask your architect for a **massing study** (1-2 weeks): how many homes really fit.
  3. Ask the town hall for the **planning report or certificate** (*informe o cédula urbanística*) in writing. Whether it is binding depends on the regional law: do not assume it is binding without checking.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** calculating with the cadastral area instead of the surveyed one; forgetting that basements or penthouses count; not checking whether a plan being processed suspends licences.

- **Before buying, also check:**
  - Whether the land needs an **additional planning or management instrument** (detailed study, reparcelling, urbanisation project). Each one adds months.
  - The required **building lines, courtyards, density and public facilities**.
  - **Flood risk** in the viewer of the National Flood Zone Mapping System (SNCZI, MITECO).
  - **Heritage protection** of the building or its surroundings (municipal catalogue).

## Lesson 1.3 — Due diligence: buying without surprises

- **Legal:**
  - *Nota simple* → owner, encumbrances (mortgages, attachments), easements, conditions subsequent, planning charges.
  - Certificate of title and encumbrances before signing.
  - **Cadastre–Registry–reality match.** If they do not match, commission a topographic survey and regularise (procedures of Ley 13/2015 reforming the mortgage and cadastral rules) before buying, or make the purchase conditional on it.
  - Tenants or occupants: contracts, terms, cost of getting them out.
  - Planning litigation or open enforcement proceedings.
  - Outstanding urbanisation charges (reparcelling instalments or compensation boards).
- **Tax:**
  - **IBI:** the property answers for unpaid IBI (real charge, art. 64 TRLRHL). Ask for a debt certificate.
  - Outstanding fees and urbanisation instalments.
- **Technical:**
  - **Topographic** survey (actual area, levels, boundaries).
  - **Preliminary geotechnical study** if you are going to build basements: the water table changes the numbers.
  - Buildings to be demolished: asbestos (RD 396/2006), demolition project and waste management (RD 105/2008).
  - Contaminated land on former industrial sites or petrol stations (Ley 7/2022 and RD 9/2005).
  - Easements for watercourses, coasts, roads, railways and airports (aeronautical easements, AESA).
  - Protected trees and archaeological sites.
- **Financial:** €/m²t paid against the market, preliminary CRP (Module 2).
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** relying only on the seller's old *nota simple*; not asking for the IBI certificate; buying with occupants and no exit plan.

- **Utility connections and services:** confirm with the companies (water, sewerage, electricity, telecoms) that there is capacity and where the connection point is. If the distribution company requires a transformer station or a network reinforcement, it can mean tens of thousands of euros and months of delay.
- **Contracts in force on the property:** leases, third-party options, rights of first refusal and pre-emption, litigation.
- **Registered, cadastral and actual area:** compare all three.

## Lesson 1.4 — The purchase: form, tax and company

- **Ways to buy:**
  - **Direct sale.**
  - **Option to buy:** you pay a premium (3-10 %) for a period (6-24 months). Register it at the Land Registry so it holds against third parties.
  - **Purchase subject to a condition precedent:** e.g. subject to obtaining the licence. Set a long-stop date and what happens to the money paid.
  - **Penitential deposit (*arras penitenciales*, art. 1454 CC):** if you pull out you lose the deposit; if the seller pulls out they pay you back double.
  - **Swap of the plot for future building:** the owner receives flats. Both deliveries are subject to IVA: go through the tax carefully with your adviser.
- **Tax on buying the plot:**

| Seller and type of land | Tax | Recoverable? |
|---|---|---|
| A business sells **buildable land** (a plot) | IVA 21 % + AJD on the deed (Annex B) | IVA yes, it is deducted. AJD no |
| A business sells **non-buildable** (rural) land | Exempt from IVA → ITP. It can waive the exemption: IVA with reverse charge + AJD | With the waiver, IVA yes |
| A business sells a **used building** to be demolished | Second delivery, exempt → ITP, or waiver of the exemption: IVA + AJD (some CCAA charge a higher AJD on a waiver) | With the waiver, IVA yes |
| A **private individual** sells | The community's ITP (Annex B). No AJD | No |

- **Costs of the purchase:**
  - Notary: by law the seller pays for the original deed and the buyer for the copy (art. 1455 CC), unless agreed otherwise. Between businesses it is negotiated.
  - Land Registry: the buyer.
  - Municipal plusvalía (IIVTNU): paid by the seller.
- **The SPE (special-purpose company):**
  - One SL per development: it ring-fences the risks, keeps the accounts tidy, and makes it easier for partners to come in and to close.
  - Minimum capital of an SL: 1 € since Ley 18/2022. Until it reaches 3.000 €, it must put 20 % of its profit into the legal reserve, and the shareholders are jointly liable if it is wound up without covering 3.000 €. No bank will finance an SL with no real equity.
  - Steps: company name (Central Companies Registry) → bank account and contribution → deed before a notary → provisional NIF (AEAT, form 036) → registration at the provincial Companies Registry → final NIF. Fast track: CIRCE / PAE.
  - In Navarra and País Vasco, IS and IVA are managed by the foral tax authority.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** buying in your own name and then contributing the land to the company (taxed twice); paying ITP when the seller could have waived the exemption and charged deductible IVA.

- [ ] Up-to-date nota simple and certificate of encumbrances
