# Plot: search, planning analysis, due diligence and acquisition

> **Objective:** buy a plot that can be built on, at the price the project can bear and with no hidden encumbrances.
> **Duration:** 120 min · **Result:** plot conveyed by deed to the SPE (project company) or an option to buy signed.
> **Where we are:** this is the first step of the development. You start with no plot and no company; in this module you find the plot, check what can be built on it and that it has no problems, and buy it in your company's name.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| TRLSRU (RDL 7/2015), art. 21 | The basic status of land: rural or urbanised. Only urbanised land with services can be built on directly |
| Regional planning law | Defines "solar" (building plot), land classification, land transfers and charges. See Annex B |
| PGOU + municipal by-laws | Buildability, heights, uses, setbacks, required parking spaces |
| Ley 12/2023 (housing), DF 4.ª | In new urbanisations: a minimum land reserve for protected housing (40 % on rural land being urbanised, 20 % in renewal schemes on urbanised land), unless regional rules say otherwise |
| Mortgage Act and Mortgage Regulation (Ley Hipotecaria y Reglamento Hipotecario) | What the Land Registry says is what counts against third parties |
| RDL 1/1993 (ITP-AJD) and Ley 37/1992 (IVA) | How the purchase is taxed |
| RDL 1/2010 (LSC) + Ley 18/2022 | Incorporating the SPE. Minimum capital of an SL: 1 € (with protection rules until it reaches 3.000 €) |
| Ley 10/2010 (money laundering) | Identifying the seller and the origin of the funds |

## Lesson 1.1 — Define the product and find the plot

> **Where we are:** you start from scratch. Before you search, you decide what you are going to develop; with that in mind, you look for plots and record them in your database.

- **Before you search, decide what you are going to develop.** It is the filter you will look at every plot through.
  - **Type of development:** open-market housing, protected housing (your autonomous community's scheme), incentivised affordable housing (Ley 12/2023) or mixed. It changes the maximum price, the IVA (4 % on protected housing under the special scheme), the financing and the buyer.
  - **Target buyers:** first home, trading up, investor, foreign buyer or second home.
  - **Approximate number of homes, unit types and floor areas.**
  - **Specification level**, garages, storerooms and common areas (pool, garden, gym, coworking).
  - **Target sale price** (€/m²) and **maximum land budget** (residual method, Module 2).
  - Result: a one-page **product sheet**.

- **Analyse the location** of each plot: transport links, public transport, services, facilities (schools, healthcare, shops) and housing demand in the area.

- **What you are looking for:** consolidated urban land, with services (road access, water, sewerage, electricity) and with set building lines. It is the only land on which you can apply for a licence without urbanising first.
- **Unconsolidated urban or developable land:** cheaper, but it needs reparcelling, land transfers and urbanisation works. Years of lead time. Not for your first development.
- **Channels, from most to least profitable:**
  1. Your own network: notaries (inheritances), lawyers, property managers, land agents, owners of old houses in areas with unused buildability.
  2. Active search in the PGOU: find blocks with unused buildability and write to the owners (address from the Cadastre).
  3. Portals: Idealista and Fotocasa (land section), Solvia, Servihabitat, Altamira, Haya, Aliseda.
  4. SAREB and bank servicers.
  5. Auctions: BOE, AEAT, Social Security, town halls.
  6. Public land: CASA47 (the state body that has replaced SEPES since 2025) and your community's land agency (Annex A).
  7. Investment funds and financial institutions selling land portfolios.
  8. Off-market deals: agreements with owners through a swap for future homes or by contributing the land to the company.
- **One plot from each channel**, as you would see them in your database:

| Channel | An example plot |
|---|---|
| Your own network | An old house from an inheritance, brought to you by a notary or a property manager |
| Active search in the PGOU | A block with unused buildability: you write to the owner at the address from the Cadastre |
| Portals | A plot advertised in the land section of Idealista or Fotocasa |
| SAREB and bank servicers | A plot from a servicer's portfolio |
| Auctions | A seized plot on the BOE Auction Portal |
| Public land | A parcel for sale or tender by CASA47 or by your community's land agency |
| Funds and financial institutions | A plot within a land portfolio |
| Off-market | An owner who swaps their plot for future homes (*permuta*) |

- **Keep your land portfolio in a database** from the first plot: each plot with its zone, classification, zoning, planning status, areas, buildability, price and payment terms, and who owns it and who brought it to you. Keep your clients too, with what they are looking for, and your land scouts (*captadores*). Use the **Professional's template · Land management database** (included): it comes with drop-down lists and a plot sheet ready to send by e-mail.
- **10-minute filter before you spend more time:**
  - Buildability × sale price in the area ÷ asking price → if the land exceeds 30 % of revenue, drop it unless the location is prime.
  - Is it open-market housing, or is there a protected-housing reserve?
  - Does anything need demolishing, or anyone need moving out?
  - To run this filter with numbers, use the **Professional's template · Plot rough numbers** (*números gordos*, included). From the buildable floor area, the land cost per square metre built (*repercusión*), the construction cost and the profit you are after, it gives you the plot price, the sale price you need and the profit. If the plot passes the filter, refine the purchase with Template 1 and the feasibility with Template 2 (Module 2).
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** falling in love with the location without running the numbers; buying "developable" land thinking it is buildable; not checking whether the town hall is changing the plan.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Throughout the course we follow **Nicolás**, who has just started out in property development. He is not a big property group: he has set up an ordinary SL to build open-market homes and sell them in Alicante, in the Comunitat Valenciana, and at every step he picks the most conventional option. Let me tell you how he got started.

**First, his product.** Before looking at a single plot, Nicolás wrote his one-page product sheet: open-market housing for first-home buyers, with 2-bedroom homes (the most in demand) and 3-bedroom ones for families (Lesson 2.2), and an upper-mid specification. With that sheet he looks at every plot through the same filter.

**Then, the search.** He started with the most profitable channel, his own network. He visited the **notaries** and the **property managers** in his area, who are the first to hear of an inheritance or of an owner who wants to sell, and left them his product sheet: what he is looking for, where and what for. He found the land agents in the list we have given you: you will find the estate agencies in your province among the potential companies for this lesson. Meanwhile, he kept an eye on the land section of Idealista and Fotocasa.

**The plot that came his way.** A property manager told him about a 344 m² plot on consolidated urban land, with services. That same day he entered it in his **land management database**, with its owner and the person who had brought it to him.

**The 10-minute filter.** He ran it through the **Plot rough numbers** template: with the zone's buildability, it comes to 1.586,49 m² of floor area in B+5 (ground floor plus five), which gives 18 homes, 2 of them penthouses. With the land at 45 € per m² of floor area, the plot comes to 71.392,05 €.

With construction at today's price, 1.400 €/m² for his upper-mid specification (Lesson 2.3), licences, design work and other costs (40 % on top of construction) and a profit of 30 % on cost, the template tells him he needs to sell at 2.606,50 € per m²: about 234.585 € for a typical 90 m² home, excluding IVA, and 4.135.186,19 € for the whole development. The land is 1,7 % of revenue, well below the 30 % the filter sets. These are the figures of the professional's example that comes with the template, except for construction, which he put at today's price (the example comes with a price from years ago), and the building you will watch go up in 3D learning. He also asked himself the filter's other two questions: it is open-market housing, with no protected-housing reserve, and nothing needs demolishing and no one needs moving out.

The numbers work for him, but he is not buying yet: first he checks in the planning documents that this can really be built (Lesson 1.2).

### Summary

First you define what you are going to develop (your product sheet); then you look for consolidated urban land through the channels on the list, record it in your database and, in 10 minutes, drop anything whose numbers do not work.

### Your checklist

- Plot entered in your land management database, with its owner and its scout
- Plot's rough numbers done before spending more time on it
- Product sheet defined (type, buyers, unit mix, specification, target price)

## Lesson 1.2 — Planning analysis: what you can build

> **Where we are:** in Lesson 1.1 you defined your product and found plots in your database that pass the 10-minute filter. Now you analyse in depth each piece of data you recorded in your template: what the planning rules say and how many homes really fit.

- **Who decides:** the autonomous community makes the planning law, and the town hall applies its PGOU and by-laws.
- **Documents you must read:**
  - **PGOU / PGM:** classification (urban, developable, rural) and zoning (residential, commercial, public facilities).
  - **Zone sheet or by-law:** the plot's parameters.
  - **Partial plan, special plan or detailed study** if the plot is inside a planning area.
  - **Heritage catalogue:** if the existing building is protected, you cannot demolish it.
- **Parameters you have to work out:**
  - **Buildability (m²t/m²s):** m² of floor area per m² of land. Example: 1.000 m² × 2 m²t/m²s = 2.000 m² of floor area.
  - **Maximum height:** in storeys (PB+4: ground floor plus four) and in metres.
  - **Maximum site coverage** at ground level (%).
  - **Setbacks** from the street front, boundaries and rear.
  - **Permitted** and compatible **uses**.
  - **Parking spaces** required (usually 1 per home or per 100 m²; it depends on the by-law).
  - **Maximum density** of homes per hectare, if there is one. It caps the number of flats.
  - **What counts** towards buildability (terraces, basements, penthouses). It varies a lot between municipalities.
- **How to do it in practice:**
  1. Download the PGOU from the municipal viewer or the regional planning viewer.
  2. Ask your architect for a **massing study** (1-2 weeks): how many homes really fit.
  3. Ask the town hall for the **planning report or certificate** (*informe o cédula urbanística*) in writing. Whether it is binding depends on the regional law: do not assume it is binding without checking.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** calculating with the cadastral area instead of the surveyed one; forgetting that basements or penthouses count; not checking whether a plan being processed suspends licences.

- **Before buying, also check:**
  - Whether the land needs an **additional planning or management instrument** (detailed study, reparcelling, urbanisation project). Each one adds months.
  - The required **building lines, courtyards, density and public facilities**.
  - **Flood risk** in the viewer of the National Flood Zone Mapping System (SNCZI, MITECO).
  - **Heritage protection** of the building or its surroundings (municipal catalogue).

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the plot in his database, Nicolás wanted to know what could really be built before committing himself. Let me tell you where he went.

**At the municipal viewer.** He downloaded the PGOU from the town hall's viewer and looked up his plot's **zone sheet**. From it he worked out the parameters: buildability, maximum height, site coverage, setbacks, uses and parking spaces. He checked very carefully what counts towards buildability, because his building has penthouses.

**With the architect.** He commissioned the **massing study** from an architecture practice in his area; you will find the ones in your province in the list we have given you, among the potential companies for this lesson. He chose the one that will later draw up the design (Module 3), so as not to start twice. Within one or two weeks, the architect confirmed what the rough numbers gave: B+5, 18 homes, 2 of them penthouses.

**At the town hall.** He went to the **planning** department and asked for the **planning report** in writing. He asked for it on the first day, because it takes time (1 to 3 months, according to the module's "Where to go" table), and he did not treat it as binding until he had checked what his community's law says.

**What he checked before going on:**

- The flood risk, in the **SNCZI** viewer (you have it among this lesson's websites).
- The **municipal heritage catalogue**: neither the plot nor its surroundings are protected.
- That no other planning instrument is needed (detailed study, reparcelling or urbanisation project), which would have added months.

Everything matches the rough numbers. Now it is time to check that the plot has no problems: the due diligence (Lesson 1.3).

### Summary

From the PGOU and the zone sheet you work out the plot's parameters; the architect tells you how many homes really fit, and the town hall gives it to you in writing. Before buying, also check flood risk, heritage and whether another planning instrument is needed.

### Your checklist

- Written planning report or certificate (informe o cédula urbanística) from the town hall
- Massing study from the architect
- Flood risk and heritage protection checked

## Lesson 1.3 — Due diligence: buying without surprises

> **Where we are:** you now know what can be built on the plot (Lesson 1.2). Before buying it, you check that it has no legal, tax, technical or financial problems: that is due diligence.

- **Legal:**
  - *Nota simple* → owner, encumbrances (mortgages, attachments), easements, conditions subsequent, planning charges.
  - Certificate of title and encumbrances before signing.
  - **Cadastre–Registry–reality match.** If they do not match, commission a topographic survey and regularise (procedures of Ley 13/2015 reforming the mortgage and cadastral rules) before buying, or make the purchase conditional on it.
  - Tenants or occupants: contracts, terms, cost of getting them out.
  - Planning litigation or open enforcement proceedings.
  - Outstanding urbanisation charges (reparcelling instalments or compensation boards).
- **Tax:**
  - **IBI:** the property answers for unpaid IBI (real charge, art. 64 TRLRHL). Ask for a debt certificate.
  - Outstanding fees and urbanisation instalments.
- **Technical:**
  - **Topographic** survey (actual area, levels, boundaries).
  - **Preliminary geotechnical study** if you are going to build basements: the water table changes the numbers.
  - Buildings to be demolished: asbestos (RD 396/2006), demolition project and waste management (RD 105/2008).
  - Contaminated land on former industrial sites or petrol stations (Ley 7/2022 and RD 9/2005).
  - Easements for watercourses, coasts, roads, railways and airports (aeronautical easements, AESA).
  - Protected trees and archaeological sites.
- **Financial:** €/m²t paid against the market, preliminary CRP (Module 2).
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** relying only on the seller's old *nota simple*; not asking for the IBI certificate; buying with occupants and no exit plan.

- **Utility connections and services:** confirm with the companies (water, sewerage, electricity, telecoms) that there is capacity and where the connection point is. If the distribution company requires a transformer station or a network reinforcement, it can mean tens of thousands of euros and months of delay.
- **Contracts in force on the property:** leases, third-party options, rights of first refusal and pre-emption, litigation.
- **Registered, cadastral and actual area:** compare all three.

- **Who handles what** (whom you ask for each check in the lists above):
  - **Land Registry:** the *nota simple*, the certificate of title and encumbrances, and the registered area.
  - **Cadastre:** the cadastral reference and area.
  - **Town hall:** at the planning department, litigation or enforcement proceedings, urbanisation charges and protected trees; at the tax office, the certificate of IBI and local-tax debts.
  - **The community's culture department:** archaeological sites.
  - **Lawyer** (usually a planning lawyer): reviews the *nota simple* and the encumbrances, the tenants or occupants, the contracts in force on the property, and the regularisation if the Cadastre, the Registry and reality do not match.
  - **Surveyor:** the topographic survey (actual area, levels, boundaries).
  - **Geotechnical laboratory:** the preliminary geotechnical study.
  - **Architect or engineering firm:** the demolition (project, asbestos with a company entered in the RERA, waste with an authorised waste manager), contaminated land, and easements for watercourses, coasts, roads, railways and airports.
  - **Utility companies:** the capacity of the connections and the connection point.
  - **You:** the €/m²t against the market and the preliminary CRP (Module 2).

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the planning clear, Nicolás did not sign anything until the due diligence was done. Let me tell you how he shared it out.

**What he did himself:**

- The **_nota simple_**, online, on the Registrars' website (it is among this lesson's websites): it is instant and costs around 9 €. There he saw the owner, the registered area and that the plot had no encumbrances.
- The **cadastral search**, on the Cadastre's online office (*Sede Electrónica del Catastro*): cadastral reference, area and plan.
- The **certificate of IBI and local-tax debts**, at the **town hall's tax office**: if the seller owes IBI, the property answers for it.
- The **utility connections**: he called the water, sewerage, electricity and telecoms companies to confirm that there was capacity and where the connection point was.

**What he commissioned:**

- The legal due diligence, from a **planning lawyer** in his province, who reviewed the *nota simple* and the encumbrances, the contracts on the property and that there were no occupants. You will find law firms in the list we have given you: among the potential companies for this lesson and, at the end of the module, through the Bar Association of your province.
- The **topographic survey**, from a surveyor, to compare the actual area with the Registry's and the Cadastre's.
- The **easements** (watercourses, coasts, roads, railways and airports), from his architect.
- Before signing, the **certificate of title and encumbrances** from the **Land Registry** (2 to 10 days): the *nota simple* is for information only; the certificate carries public faith.

**The result.** The Registry, the Cadastre and the surveyor's measurement match; there are no encumbrances, occupants, IBI debts or easements that affect it, and there is capacity for electricity and water. With the preliminary CRP (Module 2) he checked that what he is paying per m² of floor area is in line with the market. He can move on to the purchase (Lesson 1.4).

### Summary

Due diligence looks at the plot from four sides (legal, tax, technical and financial) and confirms utility connections, contracts and areas. If something does not add up, it is regularised first or the purchase is made conditional on it.

### Your checklist

- Up-to-date nota simple and certificate of encumbrances
- Cadastral reference and topographic survey match
- Certificate of no IBI or local-tax debts
- Preliminary geotechnical study if there are basements
- Utility connection capacity confirmed with the companies
- Sectoral easements reviewed (watercourses, coast, roads, heritage)
- Preliminary CRP with the maximum land price (Module 2)

## Lesson 1.4 — The purchase: form, tax and company

> **Where we are:** you have found the plot, you know what you can build (Lesson 1.2) and the due diligence has gone well (Lesson 1.3). We move on to the purchase: how it is signed, which taxes apply and in the name of which company.

- **Ways to buy:**
  - **Direct sale.**
  - **Option to buy:** you pay a premium (3-10 %) for a period (6-24 months). Register it at the Land Registry so it holds against third parties.
  - **Purchase subject to a condition precedent:** e.g. subject to obtaining the licence. Set a long-stop date and what happens to the money paid.
  - **Penitential deposit (*arras penitenciales*, art. 1454 CC):** if you pull out you lose the deposit; if the seller pulls out they pay you back double.
  - **Swap of the plot for future building:** the owner receives flats. Both deliveries are subject to IVA: go through the tax carefully with your adviser.
- **Tax on buying the plot:**

| Seller and type of land | Tax | Recoverable? |
|---|---|---|
| A business sells **buildable land** (a plot) | IVA 21 % + AJD on the deed (Annex B) | IVA yes, it is deducted. AJD no |
| A business sells **non-buildable** (rural) land | Exempt from IVA → ITP. It can waive the exemption: IVA with reverse charge + AJD | With the waiver, IVA yes |
| A business sells a **used building** to be demolished | Second delivery, exempt → ITP, or waiver of the exemption: IVA + AJD (some CCAA charge a higher AJD on a waiver) | With the waiver, IVA yes |
| A **private individual** sells | The community's ITP (Annex B). No AJD | No |

- **Costs of the purchase:**
  - **As a percentage:** notary and Land Registry, around 0,5 % of the price (an estimate, the value in Template 1; ask for a quote to refine it). Add the tax from the table above, at your community's rate (Annex B).
  - Notary: by law the seller pays for the original deed and the buyer for the copy (art. 1455 CC), unless agreed otherwise. Between businesses it is negotiated.
  - Land Registry: the buyer.
  - Municipal plusvalía (IIVTNU): paid by the seller.
- **The SPE (special-purpose company):**
  - One SL per development: it ring-fences the risks, keeps the accounts tidy, and makes it easier for partners to come in and to close.
  - Minimum capital of an SL: 1 € since Ley 18/2022. Until it reaches 3.000 €, it must put 20 % of its profit into the legal reserve, and the shareholders are jointly liable if it is wound up without covering 3.000 €. No bank will finance an SL with no real equity.
  - Steps: company name (Central Companies Registry) → bank account and contribution → deed before a notary → provisional NIF (AEAT, form 036) → registration at the provincial Companies Registry → final NIF. Fast track: CIRCE / PAE.
  - In Navarra and País Vasco, IS and IVA are managed by the foral tax authority.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** buying in your own name and then contributing the land to the company (taxed twice); paying ITP when the seller could have waived the exemption and charged deductible IVA.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the due diligence clean, Nicolás got ready to buy. Let me tell you the order he did things in.

**First, his company.** Nicolás never buys in his own name: he incorporated his SPE, an SL just for this development, and gave it real equity, because a bank will not finance an SL without it. He relied on an advisory firm (you will find one among the potential companies for this lesson) and followed the steps in the lesson:

1. The **company name**, at the Central Companies Registry (it is among this lesson's websites).
2. The **bank account** and the contribution of the capital.
3. The **deed of incorporation**, at the notary's.
4. The **provisional NIF**, at the Tax Agency (form 036).
5. **Registration** at the Companies Registry of his province, and with it the final NIF.

There is a faster route, online: CIRCE / PAE.

**Then, the option to buy.** The owner is a private individual. As Nicolás still needs the feasibility study (Module 2) and the financing, he does not buy outright: he signs an **option to buy at the notary's** and **registers it at the Land Registry**, so that it holds against third parties. He pays a premium and secures the time he needs (the lesson talks of a premium of 3-10 % for a period of 6-24 months). You will find the notary in the list we have given you: the Notarial Association of your province, at the end of the module.

**The costs, in Template 1.** As the seller is a private individual, the purchase pays the **ITP** of the Comunitat Valenciana (9 %, Annex B), not IVA: 6.425,28 € on the plot's 71.392,05 €. Notary and Land Registry, around 0,5 % of the price: 356,96 €; he asked the notary for a quote to refine it. With 3.000 € for due diligence, valuation and studies, **Template 1 · Site purchase** gives him a plot cost of 81.174,29 € (51,17 € per m² of floor area): the money he needs on the day of the purchase. The seller pays the municipal plusvalía.

**Where he is now.** He has the plot secured in his SPE's name, with the option to buy registered. With that he moves on to Module 2, to decide with numbers whether the development goes ahead.

### Summary

Buy in your company's name, never in your own; choose the form (direct sale, option, condition precedent, deposit or swap) according to what you still have to check, and look at who is selling to know which tax applies to the purchase.
