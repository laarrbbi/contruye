# Financing and contracting

> **Objective:** finance the works, hire the right contractor and cover every risk with insurance and guarantees.
> **Duration:** 120 min · **Result:** development loan signed, insurance taken out, special account for buyers' advance payments opened and construction contract signed.
> **Where we are:** with the design (Module 3) and the licence (Module 4), you finance the development and sign the contracts: the development loan, the insurance and guarantees, and the contractor. Without this the works do not start.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| LOE, **first additional provision** (*disposición adicional 1.ª*, as worded by Ley 20/2015) | Every amount you collect from a buyer before handover must be guaranteed by surety insurance or a joint and several bank guarantee and paid into a separate **special account** |
| LOE, art. 19 and **second additional provision** (*disposición adicional 2.ª*) | **Compulsory ten-year insurance** on residential buildings (exception: a self-builder of a single-family home for their own use) |
| LOE, art. 20 | Notaries and registrars do not authorise or register the completed new building without proof of the ten-year insurance |
| Orden ECO/805/2003 | Mortgage valuation of the project |
| Ley 5/2019 | The buyer's mortgage when they take over the development loan |
| Ley 32/2006 | Subcontracting: REA, at most three levels of subcontracting, subcontracting book |
| Workers' Statute (*Estatuto de los Trabajadores*), art. 42, and General Tax Act (*Ley General Tributaria*), art. 43.1.f | Liability for the contractor's Social Security and tax debts: ask for certificates that it is up to date |
| Ley 3/2004, on late payment | Maximum payment term between businesses: 60 days |
| Civil Code (*Código Civil*), arts. 1544 and 1588-1600 | The construction contract |

## Lesson 5.1 — The development loan

> **Where we are:** with the design (Module 3) and the licence (Module 4), you look for the money to build. You start with the development loan: how much the bank lends you, what it requires and how it is drawn down.

| Item | Benchmark 2025-2026 |
|---|---|
| Amount | 60–70 % of total cost (or of the finished valuation, depending on the bank) |
| Rate | 12-month Euríbor + 1,5–3 % |
| Arrangement fee | 0,5–1,5 % |
| Commitment fee | 0,1–0,5 % a year on the undrawn amount |
| Term | 24–48 months to handover, plus the take-over period |
| Drawdown | Against construction valuations |

- **What the bank requires:**
  - **Pre-sales:** 70–90 % of the homes with a private contract and an advance payment.
  - **Equity:** 30–40 % of the cost (land already paid for usually counts).
  - Approved valuation (finished-building hypothesis).
  - Mortgage over the future building (deed of new building under construction).
  - Licence, construction design, construction contract, budget, programme and CRP.
  - Policies: ten-year, TRC, RC.
  - Personal guarantees from the shareholders, for small developers.
- **Drawdown:** the director of works execution certifies each month → the bank's valuer checks progress → the bank releases the amount → you pay the contractor.
- **Take-over (*subrogación*):** at the deed, the buyer can take on their share of the loan as their own mortgage (with the protections of Ley 5/2019). If they do not, that share is cancelled with the buyer's payment.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** not negotiating the cost of the guarantees within the loan; not planning the cash between valuation and drawdown (5-15 days); signing without a clause releasing each home from the mortgage.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the licence granted, Nicolás went after the money to build. Let me tell you how he prepared his development loan.

**First, to the bank.** He took to his bank what the lesson asks for: the licence, the construction design, the budget, the programme and the CRP from his Template 2. You will find the banks in the list we have given you: among the potential companies for this lesson and, at the end of the module, the banks present in your province. The bank's **valuation company** valued the development on the finished-building hypothesis (the value for the LTV) and, with the valuation, the bank gave him its **binding offer**.

**Then, Template 3.** With the offer he filled in **Template 3 · Developer loan**, starting from the total cost in his Template 2, 3.076.443,85 €:

- The bank finances 70 %: **2.153.510,70 €**. His equity is 30 %, 922.933,16 €, and the plot, already paid for, counts within it.
- Interest at 4,5 % a year and an arrangement fee of 0,75 %: 16.151,33 €.
- 20 months of works, during which he draws down against valuations, and 4 more months until the sale deeds: 80.756,65 € of interest during the works (on half the loan, because it is drawn down little by little) and 32.302,66 € until the deeds.
- Valuation, notary and registry: 10.000 €.

Total finance cost: **139.210,64 €**, around 5 % of the cost, a little less than the 165.407,45 € he had put in Template 2.

**The pre-sales.** The bank requires 70–90 % of the homes with a private contract and an advance payment: with 18 homes, **13 at least**. You will see how he sells them in Module 8; every euro he collects will be guaranteed and will go into the special account (Lesson 5.2).

**What he negotiated.** In the same negotiation he asked for the **guarantee line** for the advance payments, with its cost within the loan. He kept cash of his own for the 5-15 days between each valuation and its drawdown. And, as he is a small developer, the bank asked him for his personal guarantee as a shareholder.

**How he will pay for the works.** Each month, the director of works execution certifies, the bank's valuer checks the progress, the bank releases the amount and Nicolás pays the contractor.

The signing is left for when he has the two documents still missing from the bank's list: the policies (Lesson 5.2) and the construction contract (Lesson 5.4).

### Summary

The development loan finances part of the cost and is drawn down against construction valuations. The bank requires pre-sales, equity, a valuation, the project documents and the policies: calculate the finance cost with Template 3 and negotiate the guarantees within the loan.

- Template 3 · Developer loan (included) filled in with the bank's offer
- Valuation and binding offer from the bank

## Lesson 5.2 — Insurance and guarantees

> **Where we are:** you have the bank's offer for the development loan (Lesson 5.1), which asks you for the policies. Now you cover every risk with insurance and guarantees: the ten-year insurance, the TRC, third-party liability and the advance payments you collect from buyers.

- **LOE warranties (art. 17)**, counted **from the date of handover of the works without reservations, or from when the reservations are remedied**:

| Period | What it covers | Who is liable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year | Finishing defects | The contractor (and the developer is liable towards the buyer) |
| 3 years | Defects affecting habitability (building services, damp, insulation) | Every party according to their role; the developer jointly and severally |
| 10 years | Structural damage that compromises strength and stability | Every party according to their role; the developer jointly and severally |

- **Ten-year insurance (compulsory on housing):**
  - Taken out by the developer. Beneficiaries: the successive owners.
  - Minimum sum insured: the final cost of the works (fees included).
  - Maximum legal excess: 1 % of the sum insured.
  - The insurer requires an **OCT** from the design stage.
  - Without it there is no deed of completed new building and no registration.
  - Cost: 0,8–1,5 % of the construction cost.
- **Three-year and one-year insurance:** provided for in the LOE but not compulsory yet.
- **Contractors' all risks (TRC):** damage to the works during construction. Not a legal requirement; the bank requires it.
- **Developer's third-party liability:** damage to third parties during the works. Advisable, and normally required by the bank.
- **Guarantee of advance payments (DA 1.ª LOE):**
  - Compulsory for every euro you collect before handing over the home: reservation, signing and payments during the works.
  - By surety insurance or a joint and several guarantee from an authorised institution, **individual for each buyer**.
  - It covers what was paid **plus statutory interest**.
  - The money goes into a separate **special account**, available only for the construction.
  - If you do not finish, or do not get first occupation (or the cédula) on time, the buyer gets their money back with interest.
  - Collecting without a guarantee is a consumer-law infringement.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Towards his buyers, Nicolás is liable over the three periods in the table (1, 3 and 10 years), counted from the handover of the works. Let me tell you how he got covered.

**Through a broker.** He went back to the insurance broker who arranged the ten-year insurer's pre-review (Module 3) and asked him for the policies compared. You will find the insurers in the list we have given you: among the potential companies for this lesson and, at the end of the module, the Association of Insurance Brokers of your province.

**The ten-year insurance.** He takes it out himself, as the developer, and its beneficiaries are the successive owners of the homes. The insurer requires an OCT from the design stage, and Nicolás already had its D0 report (Module 3). The cost, 0,8 to 1,5 % of his construction of 2.221.086 €: between 17.768,69 € and 33.316,29 €, part of the 66.632,58 € for insurance, OCT, quality control and others in his Template 2.

**The TRC and the RC.** Contractors' all risks covers damage to the works during construction: the law does not require it, the bank does. Third-party liability covers damage to third parties during the works: it is advisable and the bank normally requires it. He took out both through the broker.

**The advance payments.** Before collecting the first reservation (Module 8), he opened the **special account** at his bank, separate, available only for the construction. Each buyer will have their own guarantee, from the bank's guarantee line (Lesson 5.1). With his collections plan (Template 5, Module 8), each one will have paid him 50.541,17 € in advance before handover: 909.740,98 € across the 18 homes, guaranteed with statutory interest.

**What Nicolás does not do.** The lesson says what is compulsory; skipping it would be illegal. Nicolás never:

- collects a reservation, a payment on signing or a payment during the works without that buyer's guarantee: collecting without a guarantee is a consumer-law infringement;
- pays an advance into anything other than the special account;
- covers several buyers with a single guarantee: it is individual for each buyer and covers what was paid plus statutory interest;
- develops homes without ten-year insurance: it is compulsory, and without it there is no deed of completed new building and no registration;
- insures the works for less than their final cost, fees included, or accepts an excess of more than 1 %.

### Summary

The ten-year insurance is compulsory on housing and requires an OCT; the TRC and the RC are required by the bank. Every euro you collect before handover is guaranteed, buyer by buyer, and goes into a special account.

### Your checklist

- Special account for advance payments opened
- Ten-year insurance (with OCT), TRC and RC taken out

## Lesson 5.3 — Choosing a contractor

> **Where we are:** with the development loan under way and the insurance taken out (Lessons 5.1 and 5.2), you choose the right contractor: you prepare the tender documents, invite 3-5 contractors and compare their bids by chapter.

- **Tender documents:** construction design, measurements, specification, interim deadlines, payment terms, penalties, retentions, insurance required.
- **Invite 3-5 contractors** with:
  - Similar finished buildings (visit them and talk to the developers).
  - Annual accounts for the last three years (Companies Registry).
  - Entry in the REA.
  - Certificates that they are up to date with the AEAT and Social Security.
  - Their own team: site manager, foreman, production.
  - State contractor classification: not compulsory on private works, but a good sign of solvency.
- **Who handles what** (where you check each point in the list above):
  - **You, the developer:** you visit their similar finished buildings and talk to their developers.
  - **Companies Registry:** the annual accounts for the last three years.
  - **Labour authority** (REA public search): the entry in the REA.
  - **AEAT and Social Security (TGSS):** the certificates that they are up to date; the contractor requests them and hands them to you.
  - **The contractor** (each contractor invited): presents its own team (site manager, foreman, production) to you and, if it has one, its State contractor classification.
- **Comparison by chapter:** never compare the total alone. Look for underpriced or missing items.
- **Warning sign:** a bid 15-20 % below the average usually means mistakes, worse materials or a company that will run out of cash.

- **In each bid, also review:** exclusions, own resources, and the company's employment, health and safety, tax and insurance documents.
- **Performance guarantee:** on top of the retention, you can ask for a performance bond (usually 5–10 % of the price).

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the financing and the insurance on track, Nicolás looked for someone to build his building. Let me tell you how he chose.

**First, the tender documents.** With his architect he prepared the **tender documents**: the endorsed construction design, the measurements, the specification, the interim deadlines, the payment terms, the penalties, the retentions and the insurance required. That way they all bid for the same thing.

**Three contractors.** He asked three contractors in his area for a price; you will find the ones in your province in the list we have given you, among the potential companies for this lesson. Before opening their bids he checked each one:

- He visited similar buildings of theirs, already finished, and talked to their developers.
- He got their annual accounts for the last three years from the **Companies Registry**.
- He checked in the **REA** public search that they were entered.
- He asked them for the certificates that they are up to date with the **AEAT** and **Social Security**.
- He asked about their own team: site manager, foreman and production.

You have the Companies Registry, the REA, the AEAT and Social Security among this lesson's websites.

**The bids, chapter by chapter.** He did not look at the total: he compared each chapter, looking for underpriced or missing items, and in each bid he reviewed the exclusions, the own resources and the company's employment, health and safety, tax and insurance documents. If one had come in 15-20 % below the average, he would have gone over it with a magnifying glass: it usually means mistakes, worse materials or a company that will run out of cash.

**The one he chose.** He went with the one that had similar finished work, healthy accounts, its own team and a complete bid in every chapter: **2.221.086 €** for the whole works, what he had put in his Template 2 (1.400 €/m²). He hires it in Lesson 5.4.

### Summary

Prepare tender documents so that they all bid for the same thing, invite 3-5 contractors and check their buildings, their accounts, the REA and the certificates. Compare chapter by chapter, never just the total, and be wary of a bid far below the average.

### Your checklist

- Tender with 3-5 contractors and comparison by chapter
- REA and the contractor's AEAT and TGSS certificates

## Lesson 5.4 — The construction contract

> **Where we are:** you have now chosen the contractor (Lesson 5.3). Now you sign the construction contract with it, with its essential clauses, and prepare the start of the works.

- **Types:**
  - **Fixed price (turnkey):** certainty of cost. It needs a very well-defined design. The usual and advisable choice.
  - **By units of work (measurements):** you pay for what is actually built. More risk of overrun.
- **Clauses you cannot do without:**
  1. Purpose: the endorsed construction design as a contract document.
  2. Price, price review (if any) and valuation calendar.
  3. Payment at 60 days at most (Ley 3/2004).
  4. **Retention:** 5 % of each valuation until one year after handover, or an equivalent guarantee.
  5. Total period from the setting-out record, and intermediate milestones.
  6. **Delay penalties** (e.g. 0,05–0,2 % of the price per day, with a cap).
  7. Subcontracting: list agreed beforehand, REA compulsory, three levels at most, AEAT and TGSS certificates for each subcontractor.
  8. The contractor's insurance: RC, accidents, and its share of the TRC.
  9. Quality control and acceptance of the OCT and the laboratory.
  10. Variations: only with a written order and a price agreed **before** the work is done.
  11. Waste management (the contractor's plan).
  12. Handover, final documentation (as-built drawings, warranties, manuals) and snagging.
  13. Termination for breach and taking possession of the works.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** contracting on an incomplete design at a fixed price (every gap becomes a variation); not asking for AEAT and Social Security certificates; paying valuations without retention.

- **Preparing the start of works (site set-up):**
  - Site cabins, hoardings, access, temporary electricity and water connections, plant.
  - Health and safety plan approved.
  - The contractor's **waste management plan** (RD 105/2008, art. 5), approved by the site supervision team and accepted by the developer.
  - Laboratory and quality control hired.
  - Setting-out record and start of works.
  - Notification of the start to the town hall, the bank, the ten-year insurer and the OCT.
- **Who handles what** (who does each step of the preparation above):
  - **Contractor:** the site set-up (site cabins, hoardings, access, temporary electricity and water connections, plant), its health and safety plan and its waste management plan.
  - **Health and safety coordinator:** approves the contractor's health and safety plan (Lesson 4.3).
  - **Site supervision team:** approves the waste management plan; the works director and the director of works execution sign the setting-out record with the contractor.
  - **You, the developer:** you accept the waste management plan, hire the laboratory and the quality control, and notify the start to the town hall, the bank, the ten-year insurer and the OCT.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the contractor chosen, Nicolás signed the construction contract. Let me tell you how he closed it.

**Fixed price, turnkey.** He chose the usual and advisable option: **2.221.086 €** for the whole works. He could do so because his construction design is very well defined and was reviewed by the OCT (Module 3): with an incomplete design, every gap would have become a variation.

**Clause by clause, with his lawyer.** He went through it with a lawyer; you will find the law firms and contractors in your province in the list we have given you, among the potential companies for this lesson. What he looked at most carefully:

- The endorsed construction design, as a contract document.
- The valuation calendar (one a month, Lesson 5.1) and payment at 60 days at most.
- The **retention of 5 % of each valuation**, until one year after handover.
- The total period from the setting-out record, with its milestones, and the delay penalties, with their cap.
- Subcontracting: list agreed beforehand, REA, three levels at most and the AEAT and TGSS certificates of each subcontractor.
- The contractor's insurance: RC, accidents and its share of the TRC.
- Variations: only with a written order and the price agreed before the work is done.

**Preparing the start.** The contractor set up the site (site cabins, hoardings, access, temporary connections, plant) and submitted its waste management plan, which the site supervision team approved and Nicolás accepted. He hired the laboratory and the quality control and notified the start to the town hall, the bank, the ten-year insurer and the OCT.

**And signing the loan.** With the construction contract signed and the policies taken out, he went back to the bank with the two documents he was missing (Lesson 5.1) and signed the **development loan** and the **guarantee line**. With that, and with the order of Lesson 4.3, the works can start (Module 6).

### Summary

Contract at a fixed price, turnkey, with a very well-defined design, and do not sign without the essential clauses: retention, period, penalties, subcontracting and variations in writing. Before the first day, the site set up, the plans approved and the start notified.
