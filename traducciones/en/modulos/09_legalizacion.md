# Legalisation

> **Objective:** turn the finished works into homes that can be conveyed, mortgaged and lived in.
> **Duration:** 105 min · **Result:** first occupation (and cédula where required), deed of completed new building and horizontal division registered, Building Book.
> **Where we are:** with the works finished and handed over (Module 6), you legalise them: first occupation, the deed of completed new building and horizontal division, and the Building Book, so that each home can be sold by deed, mortgaged and lived in.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| **TRLSRU, art. 28** | Requirements for the deed and registration of a new building under construction and completed |
| **RD 1093/1997** | Rules for registering planning acts at the Land Registry |
| **LOE, arts. 7 and 20** | Building Book; ten-year insurance to execute the deed and register |
| **LPH (Ley 49/1960)**, art. 5 | Founding title of the horizontal property: shares and statutes |
| Regional planning and housing law | Licence or responsible declaration of first occupation; certificate of habitability where it still exists (Annex B) |
| **RD 390/2021** | Energy efficiency certificate of the finished building and the regional register |
| **TRLCI (RDL 1/2004)** | Entering the new building in the Cadastre |
| REBT, RITE, ICT, RIPCI | Certificates of the installations, registered |

## Where to go

| Institution | What you ask for | Indicative time |
|---|---|---|
| **Associations of Architects and Technical Architects** | Endorsement of the CFO | 1-2 weeks |
| **CCAA's Industry Service** | Registration of the installations (CIE, RITE, PCI, lift) | 1-4 weeks |
| **Regional register of energy certificates** | Registration of the finished building's certificate | 1-4 weeks |
| **Town hall** | Licence or responsible declaration of first occupation | 1-3 months (a responsible declaration takes effect at once, with a later check) |
| **Regional or island body** (in the 8 CCAA that still have it) | Certificate of habitability (*cédula de habitabilidad*) for each home | 1-2 months |
| **Notary** | Deed declaring the completed new building and the horizontal division | 1-3 weeks |
| **Land Registry** | Registration: each home becomes a property in its own right | 15 working days (extendable) |
| **Cadastre** | Entry of the new building (the notary and the registrar report it; if not, form 902-N) | 1-6 months |

## Lesson 9.1 — Technical documentation at completion

> **Where we are:** the works are finished and handed over (Module 6). Before applying for anything, you gather the technical documentation at completion, carry out the final inspection and commission the installations.

| Document | Who issues it |
|---|---|
| Final completion certificate (endorsed) | Works director + director of works execution |
| Energy efficiency certificate of the finished building (registered) | Qualified professional |
| Installation certificates registered with Industry | Authorised installers |
| ICT completion certificate and installer's certificate | Telecoms professional and installer |
| Final OCT report and final ten-year policy | OCT and insurer |
| Waste management certificates | Authorised manager |
| List of controls and tests | Director of works execution |
| As-built drawings | Site supervision team |

- **Final inspection of the works** (before applying for anything):
  - Go through every home, garage, storeroom and common area against the design, the licence and the specification.
  - Check that the building services, lifts and equipment work.
  - List the defects, snags and outstanding work, and track their close-out.
- **Tests and commissioning:**
  - Tests of the common and individual installations.
  - Certificates for the electrical, thermal and other regulated installations, with their inspection and registration.
  - Commissioning of the lift and the other equipment.
  - Permanent utility contracts and supplies.
  - Check of the building's safety, accessibility and operation.
- **Final documentation:** as-built drawings, certificates, tests, manuals, data sheets and manufacturers' warranties.
- **Who handles what** (whom you ask for each thing in the lists above):
  - **Site supervision team (works director and director of works execution):** the final inspection of the homes, garages, storerooms and common areas against the design, the licence and the specification; the list of defects and snags; the check of the building's safety, accessibility and operation; and the as-built drawings.
  - **Director of works execution:** tracking the close-out of the snags, and the list of controls and tests.
  - **Contractor:** fixing the defects and snags, and the manuals, data sheets and manufacturers' warranties for what it has installed.
  - **Authorised installers:** the tests of the common and individual installations, their certificates and the commissioning of the lift and the other equipment.
  - **CCAA's Industry Service:** the registration of the certificates of the regulated installations.
  - **Utility companies:** the permanent contracts and supplies.
  - **You (the developer):** applying for the permanent supplies, and gathering and filing all the documentation, which is the basis of the Building Book (Lesson 9.4).

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the handover record signed, Nicolás did not go to the town hall or the notary until he had all the completion papers. Let me tell you how he gathered them.

**First, the final inspection.** He went through the 18 homes and the common areas with his architect (works director), his technical architect (director of works execution) and the contractor's site manager, with the design, the licence and the specification in hand: the same specification he gave his buyers, because the advertising binds him (Lesson 8.1). They noted every defect and every snag on a list; the contractor fixed them and the director of works execution checked that they were closed out.

**Tests and commissioning.** The installers tested the common installations and those of each home, and commissioned the lift and the equipment. He had applied in good time to the distributor for the permanent electricity connection, because it takes months (Lesson 9.3 warns of it).

**The papers, each from whoever issues it** (the lesson's table):

- The **CFO**, signed by his architect and his technical architect and endorsed at the **Associations of Architects and Technical Architects** (1 to 2 weeks).
- The installation certificates, from the authorised installers, registered with the **CCAA's Industry Service** (1 to 4 weeks).
- The **energy efficiency certificate** of the finished building, which his architect signed as the qualified professional, entered in the **regional register of energy certificates**: in the Comunitat Valenciana, the IVACE (Lesson 8.3).
- The ICT completion certificate, from the telecoms professional and the installer.
- The **OCT's final report**. With it, the insurer issued him the **final ten-year policy**, through his broker.
- The waste manager's certificates, the director of works execution's list of controls and tests, and the site supervision team's as-built drawings.

You have your province's Industry Service and register of energy certificates at the end of the module.

**Everything, into one folder.** Drawings, certificates, tests, manuals, data sheets and manufacturers' warranties: it is the basis of the Building Book (Lesson 9.4). With that he can now apply for first occupation and prepare the Final Completion Deed.

### Summary

Before applying for first occupation or going to the notary, check the whole of the works against the design, test and commission the installations, and gather each document signed by whoever issues it: the endorsed CFO, the installations and the energy certificate registered, and the OCT's final report with the final ten-year policy.

### Your checklist

- CFO endorsed
- Building services registered with Industry
- Energy certificate registered
- Final ten-year insurance policy
- Final inspection and commissioning of building services

## Lesson 9.2 — Declaration of new building and horizontal division

> **Where we are:** you have the technical documentation at completion (Lesson 9.1). Now you take it to the notary and the Land Registry: the completed new building and the horizontal division, so that each home becomes a property in its own right.

- **New building under construction** (at the start): licence + the professional's certificate that the description matches the design. It lets the bank take a mortgage over the future building.
- **Completed new building** (or completion record), art. 28 TRLSRU. You need:
  - The professional's final completion certificate.
  - Licence or verification of first occupation (or responsible declaration), depending on the regional law.
  - Ten-year insurance (art. 20 LOE).
  - Energy efficiency certificate.
  - Building Book, where the regional law requires it for registration.
- **Horizontal division (founding title, art. 5 LPH):**
  - Describes the building and each private unit (home, commercial unit, garage, storeroom) with number, floor, area, boundaries and **participation share**.
  - Common elements.
  - The owners' association's **statutes** (use of terraces, pool, sharing of costs, prohibited uses).
  - It goes in the same deed as the new building: the deed declaring the new building and the horizontal division, which is executed at the start of the works (Lesson 10.3).
- **After registration:** each home is a separate registered property and can be sold and mortgaged on its own. The bank spreads the development loan's mortgage across the properties.
- **Cost:** notary and registry per official scale, AJD (Annex B) on the value of the new building and the horizontal division. **It cannot be passed on to a consumer buyer.**

- **Regularise** in the deed the areas, appurtenances and common elements if what was built differs from the original design, and check that the resulting properties match the drawings and titles.
- **Energy label:** get it and give it to the buyers; it is compulsory when offering and selling (RD 390/2021).

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Nicolás signed the deed declaring the new building and the horizontal division at the start of the works (Lesson 4.3), and the Land Registry registered it with the "under construction" note (Lesson 10.3). It already included the building and its 18 homes, each with its number, floor, area, boundaries and participation share, the common elements and the owners' association's statutes, which he prepared with his lawyer. With the works finished, it is time for the Final Completion Deed. Let me tell you how he did it.

**First, checking.** With his architect he checked that what was built matches the design and that the resulting properties match the drawings and the titles. There were no areas, appurtenances or common elements to regularise.

**What he took to the notary.** What the lesson asks for the completed new building, with the six documents of Lesson 10.3:

1. The Building Book in digital form, with the handover record of the works (Lesson 9.4).
2. The endorsed final completion certificate (Lesson 9.1).
3. The ten-year insurance: without it, the notary does not authorise the completed new building and the registrar does not register it.
4. The first occupation licence (I tell you how he got it in Lesson 9.3).
5. The energy efficiency certificate.
6. The georeferenced areas, which his architect prepared for him with the surveyor from Lesson 1.3.

**Signing and registration.** He signed the Final Completion Deed at his notary's, who filed it at the **Land Registry** (15 working days, extendable). It was registered as a completed new building and the "under construction" note disappeared. From that day each of his 18 homes is a registered property in its own right. You will find the notary and the lawyer in the list we have given you, among the potential companies for this lesson.

**The Cadastre.** The entry of the new building is reported by the notary and the registrar; if not, form 902-N is filed. Nicolás checked on the Cadastre's online office (*Sede Electrónica del Catastro*) that it had been done (it can take 1 to 6 months).

**What he pays.** The notary and the registry, per official scale, and his community's AJD (Annex B) on the value of the new building and the horizontal division. They are his costs: he does not pass them on to his buyers. And he gives each of them the energy label of the finished building.

### Summary

The completed new building is declared at the notary's with the final completion certificate, first occupation, the ten-year insurance, the energy certificate and the rest of what the notary asks for, and it is registered at the Registry: each home becomes a property in its own right. Its costs are the developer's and are not passed on to the consumer buyer.

### Your checklist

- Deed of completed new building and horizontal division signed and registered
- Cadastre updated

## Lesson 9.3 — First occupation licence

> **Where we are:** in Lesson 9.2 you saw that the deed of completed new building needs first occupation. Now you apply for it at the town hall: it certifies that the building matches the licence and can be used.

- **First occupation (town hall):** certifies that the building matches the licence and can be used.
  - Usual documents: CFO, installation certificates, energy certificate, photos, Cadastre entry or proof of filing, certificate of the utility connections, final ICIO assessment.
- **Who handles what** (whom you ask for each document in the list above):
  - **Works director and director of works execution:** the CFO, endorsed at their associations.
  - **Authorised installers:** the installation certificates, registered with the CCAA's Industry Service.
  - **Qualified professional:** the energy certificate, entered in the regional register of energy certificates.
  - **Cadastre:** the entry of the new building or proof of it (the notary and the registrar report it; if not, form 902-N).
  - **Utility companies:** the certificate of the utility connections.
  - **Town hall:** the final ICIO assessment, which you pay.
  - **You (the developer):** the photos, and the application, with everything on this list, at the town hall.
- **Certificate of habitability (*cédula de habitabilidad*):** it is the same document as the first occupation licence; they are not different acts or documents. For subsequent transfers it would be a second occupation licence. In 2026 it still exists in **Cataluña, Navarra, Canarias, Extremadura, Illes Balears, La Rioja, Asturias and Región de Murcia**. It is issued by the regional or island body and is usually needed to sign permanent utility contracts and to sell.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** applying for first occupation with unregistered installations; not having the permanent electricity connection done (the distributor takes months); forgetting the cédula in the communities that require it.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the completion papers gathered (Lesson 9.1), Nicolás went for first occupation. Let me tell you how he did it.

**First, to the town hall.** With the works finished, the **first occupation licence** is granted by the town hall: he went to his town hall's **planning** department to apply for it.

**The application, complete.** He filed it at the town hall with:

- The endorsed CFO.
- The installation certificates, already registered with the Industry Service: applying with unregistered installations is the lesson's first typical mistake.
- The energy certificate of the finished building, entered in the register.
- The photos of the finished building.
- The Cadastre entry or proof of it (you have the Cadastre's online office among this lesson's websites).
- The certificate of the utility connections, with the permanent electricity connection already done.
- The final ICIO assessment, which he paid at the town hall itself.

**The time it takes.** For the licence, 1 to 3 months, according to the "Where to go" table. Nicolás had allowed for it in his programme, because without first occupation there is no Final Completion Deed (Lesson 9.2), and in the Comunitat Valenciana his buyers need it to contract permanent water and electricity (Lesson 8.3).

You have the town hall and the rest of your province's institutions for this phase at the end of the module.

### Summary

The first occupation licence certifies before the town hall that the building matches the licence and can be used. Apply for it with the application complete, the installations registered and the permanent connection done.

### Your checklist

- First occupation (licence or responsible declaration)
- Certificate of habitability (if your CCAA requires it)

## Lesson 9.4 — The Building Book

> **Where we are:** with the completion documentation (Lesson 9.1) and first occupation (Lesson 9.3), you bring it all together in the Building Book: what has been built, who took part and how the building is used and maintained.

- **Compulsory** (art. 7 LOE), and regulated in more detail by regional decrees.
- **Contents:**
  1. The design with its approved changes.
  2. List of the parties involved.
  3. Licences, insurance and warranties.
  4. Installation and test certificates.
  5. Use and maintenance instructions for the building and its installations.
  6. Maintenance plan with the compulsory inspections (lift, PCI, thermal, electrical).
- **Who handles what** (who gives you each part of the contents above):
  - **Works director (your architect):** the design with the approved changes and the use and maintenance instructions for the building, which go in the documentation of the works as built that they hand you at the end (Lesson 6.5).
  - **Director of works execution (your technical architect):** the test certificates, with the list of controls and tests.
  - **Authorised installers:** the installation certificates and the use and maintenance instructions for each installation.
  - **Site supervision team, with the installers:** the maintenance plan with the compulsory inspections.
  - **Contractor:** the warranties for the equipment and materials it has installed.
  - **Town hall:** the licences.
  - **Ten-year insurer:** the ten-year insurance.
  - **You (the developer):** the list of the parties involved, and gathering it all in the Book and handing it over.
- **Index of the Building Book** (**Instructor's template · Building Book**, included, in Word to fill in):
  - **Part I. Characteristics of the building.**
    - For the building as a whole:
      - Description: site plan; photographs of the façades; characteristics of the foundations and structure; characteristics of the envelope and partitions; characteristics of the roof; description and layout of the installations; characteristics of the coverings; energy performance and efficiency of the building; performance against noise and vibration.
      - Companies and professionals involved: developers; authors of the design; site supervision team; consultants, advisers and collaborators; contractor; subcontractors and installers; other parties.
      - Quality accreditation.
      - History of the works.
      - List of the occupancy units.
    - For each occupancy unit:
      - Identification and description of the units.
      - History of the interventions in each occupancy unit.
  - **Part II. Rules and instructions for use, upkeep and maintenance.**
    - Use and maintenance manual.
    - Log of maintenance and repair operations.
    - Summary of the Book for the owners' association.
  - **Part III. Rules of action in the event of an accident or in emergency situations.**
    - For the building as a whole.
    - For each occupancy unit.
- **Handing it over:** the developer gives it to the owners' association (chair or property manager). Each buyer gets the part for their home.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Nicolás did not start the Building Book from scratch: he used the **Instructor's template · Building Book**, the Word index you have below this lesson, and filled it in with his architect (works director) and his technical architect (director of works execution). Let me tell you how they shared out the work.

**The basis.** At the end of the works, the works director handed him the documentation of the works as built: the design with its changes, the list of parties, the use and maintenance instructions and the certificates (Lesson 6.5). With that and the completion folder (Lesson 9.1) they had almost everything.

**Part I, characteristics of the building.**

- **The description** of the building as a whole (site, photographs of the façades, foundations and structure, envelope and partitions, roof, installations, coverings, energy efficiency, noise and vibration) was written by the architect from the design.
- **The companies and professionals involved** were entered by Nicolás, with their contracts: his SPE as developer, the architecture practice and the engineering firms it coordinated, the site supervision team, the contractor with its subcontractors and installers, and the other parties, such as the OCT and the laboratory.
- **The quality accreditation and the history of the works**, from the director of works execution: controls, tests, records and certificates.
- **The occupancy units:** his 18 homes, 2 of them penthouses, each with its identification, its description and its history, with the changes its buyer asked for in the contract appendix (Lesson 8.3).

**Part II, use, upkeep and maintenance.** The use and maintenance manual, with the compulsory inspections of the lift, the PCI and the thermal and electrical installations, was prepared by the architect and the technical architect with the installers' instructions. The log of maintenance operations is left ready for the owners' association to keep, with the summary of the Book for the owners' association.

**Part III, accidents and emergencies**, for the building as a whole and for each home.

**Handing it over.** He kept a copy in digital form with the handover record, for the notary (Lesson 10.3). The complete Book is for the owners' association, its chair or its property manager; he gives each buyer the part for their home at the signing of the sale (Lesson 11.1). Before that, he checked with his architect what his autonomous community's decree adds.

### Summary

The Building Book is compulsory: it brings together the design with its changes, who took part, licences, insurance, warranties, certificates and how the building is used and maintained. Fill in the instructor's template with your site supervision team and hand it over to the owners' association, with each home's part for its buyer.

### Your checklist

- Building Book complete

## Lesson 9.5 — Warranty file

> **Where we are:** you have the Building Book complete (Lesson 9.4). To close the legalisation you prepare the warranty file: the ten-year insurance, the warranties for equipment and materials, the liabilities of each party and how you will deal with incidents after handover.

- **Complete the ten-year insurance:** final policy after the OCT's final report.
- **Collect the warranties** for installations, equipment and materials (aerothermal heat pumps, lift, windows, waterproofing).
- **Document the liabilities** of each party to the building process: list of parties and their contracts (art. 7 LOE).
- **Prepare the warranty file** for the buyers and for the owners' association.
- **Define the claims procedure** that will apply after handover (Module 12).

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Before handing over the keys, Nicolás wanted to have in a single file whom to call if something goes wrong. Let me tell you what he put in it.

**The ten-year insurance.** The final policy, which the insurer issued him after the OCT's final report (Lesson 9.1). Its number and the insurer go on the certificate each buyer will receive (Lesson 10.2).

**The warranties for equipment and materials.** He asked the contractor for them at the handover of the works: those for the lift, the windows, the waterproofing and the rest of the equipment, each with its manufacturer or its installer and its period.

**Who is liable for what.** The list of parties with their written contracts: the architect and the technical architect of the site supervision team, the health and safety coordinator, the contractor, the OCT and the laboratory. Next to it, the legal warranty periods, counted from handover without reservations (Lesson 5.2): 1 year for finishes, the contractor's; 3 years for habitability and 10 for the structure, every party's according to their role. Nicolás, as developer, is liable towards the buyer for all of them, jointly and severally for the 3 and 10-year ones.

**For whom.** One file for the owners' association, with the common areas and the common installations, and another for each buyer, with what concerns their home, which is added to the file he opened at the reservation (Lesson 8.4).

**The claims procedure.** He left it in writing for after handover: a single channel, each incident with its number, its classification by warranty period and the written instruction to the contractor or the party responsible. If they do not respond, he repairs it himself and charges it to the 5 % retention on each valuation, which he keeps until one year after handover (Lesson 5.4). He will put it into practice in Module 12.

### Summary

Close the legalisation with a warranty file: the final ten-year policy, the warranties for equipment and materials, the list of parties with their liabilities and the claims procedure for after handover, for each buyer and for the owners' association.

### Your checklist

- Warranty file prepared
