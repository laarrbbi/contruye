# Pre-handover

> **Objective:** every buyer receives a home without defects, and the notary has everything ready before signing day.
> **Duration:** 70 min · **Result:** snagging lists closed, handover documentation for each home and signing calendar.
> **Where we are:** with the building legalised (Module 9), you prepare the handover: you go through each home with its buyer, prepare its documents and get the notary ready for the signings.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| LOE, art. 17 | Whatever is not snagged now comes back later as a warranty claim |
| TRLGDCU, art. 89.3 | Costs you cannot pass on to the buyer |
| Notarial Regulation (*Reglamento Notarial*), art. 126 | The consumer buyer chooses the notary |
| Ley 5/2019, arts. 14 and 15 | The buyer's mortgage: the bank gives them the documents (FEIN, FiAE) at least 10 calendar days beforehand, and the buyer appears before the notary for the preliminary record at least the day before signing |
| Ley 10/2010 | Identification and origin of funds before signing |

## Where to go

| Who | What you ask for |
|---|---|
| **Contractor** | Snags fixed on time (charged to the retention if it does not comply) |
| **Notary** | Signing calendar, draft deed, provision for costs |
| **The buyers' banks** | Signing date for each mortgage |
| **Town hall** | Cadastral values and data for each home's plusvalía |

## Lesson 10.1 — Snagging

> **Where we are:** the building is legalised (Module 9) and the handover is approaching. You start with the snagging: three rounds so that each buyer receives a home without defects.

- **Round 1 · internal (6-8 weeks before):** the developer's professional + the site manager go through each home with a snagging sheet per room. The contractor fixes.
- **Round 2 · buyer (3-4 weeks before):** guided visit, the buyer notes their defects, the pre-handover sheet is signed with a deadline for fixing.
- **Round 3 · verification:** final check before signing.
- **What to check:**

| Area | Points |
|---|---|
| Finishes | Knocks on walls and ceilings, paint, floor joints, scratches on floors and glass |
| Internal joinery | Doors closing, ironmongery, wardrobes |
| External joinery | Windows closing and watertight, shutters, sealants |
| Electrical | Every light point and socket, RCDs, video entry phone |
| Plumbing | Pressure, hot water, drains, traps, no leaks |
| HVAC | Aerothermal heat pump, splits or underfloor heating working |
| Damp | Ceilings under terraces and roof, basements |
| Telecoms | Sockets and fibre in each room |
| Common areas | Lift, lighting, garage doors, landscaping |

- **Tip:** use an app or a sheet with photos and close each point with a date and a signature.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the building legalised, Nicolás did not want any buyer to find a defect on the day the keys were handed over. Let me tell you how he organised the snagging.

**First, the sheet.** He prepared a snagging sheet per room for each of the 18 homes, with the areas in the lesson's table: finishes, joinery, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, damp, telecoms and common areas. He kept it in an app with photos, to close each point with a date and a signature.

**Round 1, the internal one.** Between six and eight weeks before handover he went through each home with his technical architect, the director of works execution, who acted as the developer's professional, and with the contractor's site manager. He looked most carefully at the ceilings under the roof and the terraces, which is where damp appears; the building has no basement. The contractor fixed everything they noted.

**Round 2, with each buyer.** Three or four weeks before, he invited each buyer to a guided visit. The buyer noted their defects and the two of them signed the pre-handover sheet, with its deadline for fixing.

**Round 3, the verification.** Before each signing he checked, point by point, that the sheet was closed.

**Why so much care.** The module's "Laws that apply" table says it: whatever is not snagged now comes back later as a warranty claim. And the "Where to go" table reminds him what to ask the contractor for: the snags fixed on time and, if it does not comply, charged to the retention. Nicolás still held back 5 % of each valuation (Lesson 5.4), so he could have commissioned the fixing from another company. You will find the finishing companies in your province in the list we have given you: among the potential companies for this lesson. He did not need to: the contractor met the deadlines.

With the three rounds closed, he moves on to preparing the papers for each home (Lesson 10.2).

### Summary

You snag each home in three rounds: an internal one with the site manager, another with the buyer, who signs the pre-handover sheet with its deadline for fixing, and a final verification before signing. Each point is closed with a date, a signature and a photo.

### Your checklist

- Three snagging rounds closed

## Lesson 10.2 — Documentation for each home

> **Where we are:** with the homes snagged (Lesson 10.1), you prepare the handover documentation for each one and check that each buyer has made their payments before calling them in.

| Document | Note |
|---|---|
| Copy of the deed (after signing) | The notary provides it |
| Ten-year policy certificate | Policy number and insurer |
| Equipment warranties | Aerothermal heat pump, appliances if any |
| Use and maintenance manual | Extract from the Building Book |
| Plans of the home | With areas and the position of the consumer unit and stopcocks |
| Energy efficiency certificate and label | For the finished building |
| Certificate of habitability | In the CCAA that require it |
| Owners' association details | Statutes, share, property manager |
| Utility details | Electricity CUPS, water meter, CIE |
| Sets of keys and remotes | Home, entrance, storeroom, garage |

- **For each buyer, before calling them in:** confirm they have made the payments under the contract, and review the position on payments, taxes and costs that fall to each party.
- **Complete the handover file** with: registry identification of the home and its appurtenances; access cards and codes; the association's rules, property manager's details and expected service charges; inspection record with incidents and commitments to fix them.
- **During the visit,** explain how the equipment, HVAC, ventilation and home automation (if any) work.
- **Who handles what** (whom you ask for each document in the table and the file above):
  - **Notary:** the copy of the deed, after signing.
  - **Land Registry:** the registry identification of the home and its appurtenances (each home has been a property in its own right since the horizontal division, Lesson 9.2).
  - **Ten-year insurer:** the policy certificate, with its number.
  - **Works director and director of works execution (the site supervision team):** the plans of the home, taken from the as-built drawings (Lesson 9.1).
  - **Qualified professional:** the energy efficiency certificate and label of the finished building.
  - **Contractor:** the equipment warranties, the sets of keys and remotes and the access cards and codes (Lesson 6.5), and fixing the incidents in the inspection record.
  - **Electrical installer and utility companies:** the CIE, registered with Industry; the electricity CUPS and the water meter.
  - **Property manager:** the property manager's details and the expected service charges.
  - **You (the developer):** the use and maintenance manual (an extract from the Building Book), the association's statutes and rules, the check of each buyer's payments, the inspection record and the explanation during the visit.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the homes snagged, Nicolás prepared a folder for each of the 18. Let me tell you where he got each paper from.

**What he already had from Module 9.** From the **Building Book** he put together with the instructor's template (Lesson 9.4) he took, for each home, its use and maintenance manual. The energy efficiency certificate and label of the finished building, already registered, served for all 18.

**What he asked for:**

- The **plans of each home**, with areas and the position of the consumer unit and stopcocks, from his architect, taken from the as-built drawings.
- The **ten-year policy certificate**, with its number and the insurer, from his insurance broker.
- The **equipment warranties** and the sets of keys for the home and the entrance, from the contractor, which handed them over with its final documentation (Lesson 6.5).
- The **utility details**: the CIE of the electrical installation, the CUPS of each home and its water meter.
- The **owners' association details**: the statutes in his horizontal division deed, each home's share and the property manager he was going to propose to the meeting (Lesson 11.2).

**Before calling anyone in, the payments.** In his CRM (Module 8) he checked that each buyer had made what their contract required. For a home at the average price, according to his Template 5: the reservation of 6.000 €, the rest of the 10 % on signing the private contract (19.270,58 €) and the 18 instalments of 1.403,92 € during the works. In total, 50.541,17 € with IVA. He also went over which payments, taxes and costs fall to each party; you will see them in Lesson 11.1.

**The handover file.** He completed each folder with the registry identification of the home, the access cards and codes, the association's rules with the expected service charges and the inspection record from Lesson 10.1, with its incidents and commitments to fix them. And during the visit he explained to each buyer how the equipment, the HVAC and the ventilation work.

With the 18 folders ready, all he has left is the notary (Lesson 10.3).

### Summary

Each home has its handover folder: ten-year insurance, warranties, use manual, plans, energy certificate, owners' association and utility details, keys and, after signing, the copy of the deed. Before calling a buyer in, check that they have made their payments.

### Your checklist

- Handover documentation prepared for each home

## Lesson 10.3 — The notary

> **Where we are:** with the homes snagged and their documentation prepared (Lessons 10.1 and 10.2), you get the notary ready: the Final Completion Deed and, 3-4 weeks before signing, each buyer's date and settlement statement.

- **At the start of the works,** the building is first documented by executing before a notary the corresponding public deed of "Declaration of New Building and Horizontal Division" (*Declaración de Obra Nueva y División Horizontal*), which is filed at the Land Registry for registration. The Registry registers it with the note "Under construction" (*En construcción*).
- **When the works are finished,** the **Final Completion Deed** (*Escritura Final de Obra*) is executed and filed at the Land Registry for registration. It is registered as a completed new building, and the "under construction" note disappears.
- **For the Final Completion Deed,** the following documents must be presented at the notary's:
  1. The Building Book in digital form (it must expressly include the handover record of the works). Its index, in Lesson 9.4.
  2. Final completion certificate.
  3. Ten-year insurance.
  4. First occupation licence.
  5. Energy efficiency certificate.
  6. Georeferenced areas.
- **3-4 weeks before:**
  - The buyer chooses the notary (a consumer right). Offer yours, without imposing it.
  - Prepare the **settlement statement** for each buyer: price, payments received with IVA, amount outstanding.
- **The buyer brings:** DNI or NIE, documents on the origin of the funds, proof of earlier payments, and their mortgage, which is signed the same day (with the documents received at least 10 calendar days beforehand and the notary's preliminary record done at least the day before, Ley 5/2019).
- **Means of payment:** the notary identifies each payment (named cheque or bank transfer). Nothing in cash above the legal limit.
- **Who handles what** (whom you ask for each thing in the lists above):
  - **You (the developer):** the Building Book in digital form, with the handover record of the works, based on the documentation the works director hands you (Lesson 6.5); each buyer's settlement statement; offering your notary, without imposing it.
  - **Works director and director of works execution:** the final completion certificate, endorsed at their associations (Lesson 9.1).
  - **Ten-year insurer:** the ten-year insurance, with the final policy after the OCT's final report (Lesson 9.5).
  - **Town hall:** the first occupation licence.
  - **Qualified professional:** the energy efficiency certificate, entered in the regional register.
  - **Surveyor:** the georeferenced areas.
  - **Notary:** the Final Completion Deed and, on signing day, the identification of each payment.
  - **Land Registry:** the registration as a completed new building, without the "under construction" note.
  - **The buyer:** chooses the notary, brings their DNI or NIE, the origin of their funds and the proof of their payments, and does the preliminary record for their mortgage before the notary at least the day before.
  - **The buyer's bank:** the signing date of the mortgage and its documents, at least 10 calendar days beforehand.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the folders prepared, Nicolás went back to his notary, the one he chose in Lesson 1.4. Let me tell you the two steps he took there.

**The Final Completion Deed, already registered.** The building went through this notary's office twice. At the start, with the deed declaring the new building and the horizontal division, which the **Land Registry** registered with the "under construction" note. At the end, in Module 9 (Lesson 9.2), with the Final Completion Deed and the six documents of this lesson: the Building Book in digital form with the handover record, the final completion certificate, the ten-year insurance, the first occupation licence, the energy efficiency certificate and the georeferenced areas. The Registry registered it as a completed new building and the "under construction" note disappeared. He checked this before calling anyone in, because while that note remains a buyer's bank will not formalise their mortgage (Lesson 4.3).

**The signings, three or four weeks before.** He offered his notary to each buyer, without imposing it, because choosing the notary is a consumer right. With each buyer's notary and with the banks of the buyers with a mortgage he confirmed the dates and closed the signing calendar. He reminded those buyers of the deadlines: their bank's documents, at least 10 calendar days beforehand, and the preliminary record before the notary, at least the day before.

**A settlement statement for each buyer.** For a home at the average price: price, 252.705,83 € with IVA; payments received, 50.541,17 € with IVA; amount outstanding, 202.164,66 €, which he will collect at the deed (Lesson 11.3).

**Money laundering.** The developer is an obliged entity. Nicolás asked each buyer for their DNI or NIE, the documents on the origin of their funds and the proof of their earlier payments, and told them they would pay by named cheque or bank transfer: nothing in cash above the legal limit. You will find the notaries in your province in the list we have given you: among the potential companies for this lesson and, at the end of the module, through the Notarial Association.

### Summary

The building goes through the notary's office twice: at the start, the declaration of new building and horizontal division, registered "under construction"; at the end, the Final Completion Deed with its six documents, which registers it as completed. Before the signings, each buyer chooses the notary, receives their settlement statement and evidences their funds.
