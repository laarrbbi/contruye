# Site control

> **Objective:** finish the works on time, on budget and with the documented quality that the bank, the insurer and the buyers will ask you for.
> **Duration:** 100 min · **Result:** control system with valuations, signed variations, monthly report and complete quality documentation.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| LOE, arts. 12-14 | Duties of the works director, the director of works execution, and the control bodies and laboratories |
| CTE, Part I, art. 7 and annex II | Acceptance control of products, execution control, control of the finished building; record of the checks |
| RD 410/2010 | Requirements for quality control bodies and testing laboratories (responsible declaration to the CCAA) |
| Structural Code (Código Estructural, RD 470/2021) | Control of concrete and steel |
| Decreto 462/1971 | Orders and attendance book |
| RD 1627/1997, art. 13 | The safety coordinator's incidents book |
| Construction contract | Valuations, variations, penalties |

## Where to go

| Who | What they give you |
|---|---|
| **Site supervision team** (works director + director of works execution) | Site instructions, monthly valuations, quality control |
| **Accredited laboratory** | Tests on concrete, steel, watertightness |
| **OCT** | Inspection reports and the final report the insurer needs |
| **The bank's valuer** | Check of each valuation before the loan is drawn |
| **The developer's project manager** (in-house or external) | Control of cost, time and variations |

## Lesson 7.1 — The site supervision team

| Role | Qualification on housing | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Works director | Architect | Directs the technical development, answers design queries, signs the CFO |
| Director of works execution | Technical architect | Controls execution, the quality of materials and tests, measures and **certifies** the work, signs the CFO |
| Safety coordinator during the works | Qualified professional | Approves the safety plan, coordinates the companies, keeps the incidents book |

- **Tools:**
  - Orders and attendance book: every visit and every instruction in writing.
  - Incidents book (safety).
  - Minutes of the weekly meeting.
  - Monthly report to the developer.
- **Require in the contract** a minimum number of visits and that site queries are answered within 48-72 h.

## Lesson 7.2 — The OCT and quality control

- **The OCT is not required by law:** the ten-year insurer requires it. No OCT, no ten-year insurance; no ten-year insurance, no deed of completed new building.
- **What it reviews:**
  - At design stage (D0 report): structure, foundations, waterproofing.
  - On site: foundations, structure (reinforcement before pouring), roofs and basements.
  - At the end: closing report for the insurer.
- **Examples of OCTs in Spain:** Bureau Veritas, Applus+, SGS, Eurocontrol, OCA, among others.
- **Control laboratory:** test specimens from each batch according to the control plan, strength at 28 days (some series also at 7 days to get an early reading), steel with the manufacturer's certificate, watertightness and building services tests.
- **File everything:** concrete delivery notes, test reports, certificates of CE-marked products. The OCT, and later the Building Book, will ask for them.

- **Non-conformities:** record each one (what, where, who found it), order the remedy and close it with evidence (photo, test, record).
- Keep the **record of the final tests** with the rest of the quality documentation.

## Lesson 7.3 — Cost control

- **Your control budget:** the contract budget by chapter + a contingency reserve (3-5 %).
- **Every month:**
  1. The director of works execution measures and certifies.
  2. You check the valuation against the budget by chapter.
  3. You apply the 5 % retention.
  4. The bank's valuer checks it.
  5. You pay on time (60 days at most).
- **Variations:**
  - Nothing is built without a **written order** signed by the developer and the site supervision team and a **price agreed beforehand**.
  - Each variation updates the "cost at completion".
- **Typical overruns:**

| Cause | Typical impact |
|---|---|
| Developer's changes | +3–8 % |
| Ground (water, rock, made ground) | +2–10 % |
| Specification upgrades under sales pressure | +2–5 % |
| Design errors or omissions | +1–3 % |
| Rising material prices | variable |

- **Three figures to look at every month:** **budgeted** cost, **committed** cost (contracted + orders + approved variations) and **actual** cost (certified and paid).
- **New rates (*precios contradictorios*):** the price of a unit of work that was not in the budget. Agreed in writing before it is carried out.
- Control **retentions, advances and payments**, and also taxes, fees, interest and overheads.
- Update the **forecast of final cost and cash** every month.

## Lesson 7.4 — Programme and reporting

- **Programme (Gantt):** the contractor prepares it, the site supervision team approves it, you check it every week. Identify the **critical path**: if an activity on that path slips, the whole job slips.
- **Meetings:**

| Meeting | Frequency | Attendees |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Weekly | Site supervision team, site manager, the developer's project manager |
| Cost | Monthly | Developer, project manager, site supervision team |
| Building services | Fortnightly | Site supervision team and installers |

- **KPIs in the monthly report:**

| KPI | Formula |
|---|---|
| Physical progress | Value built ÷ value contracted |
| Financial progress | Certified ÷ contracted |
| Time deviation | Days against the programme |
| Cost at completion | Contracted + variations + allowances |
| Updated margin | Forecast revenue − forecast total cost |
| Sales | Units sold and paid for against the plan |

- **Delays:** anticipate the usual ones (supplies, weather, permits, incidents) and take corrective action. Document every **extension of time** in writing: it affects penalties, the loan and the buyers.
- **Financial and sales follow-up during the works:**
  - Meet the loan's drawdown conditions and support each valuation before the bank and its valuer.
  - Update the forecasts of sales, collections and debt repayment.
  - Coordinate the handover calendar with sales.
  - Keep control of liquidity until the final settlement of the development.
