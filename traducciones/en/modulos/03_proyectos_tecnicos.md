# Technical design

> **Objective:** have a design that gets the licence, can be built at the planned price and is accepted by the ten-year insurer.
> **Duration:** 110 min · **Result:** basic design and construction design, with the health and safety study, geotechnical study and waste management.
> **Where we are:** with the GO from Module 2, you commission the technical designs: the architect and their team turn the fit study into the basic design, with which you apply for the licence, and the construction design, with which the building is built. Nothing is being built yet.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| LOE (Ley 38/1999), arts. 4-16 | What a design is and what each party does. For housing, the designer and the works director are **architects** (art. 10.2.a and 12.3.a) |
| CTE (RD 314/2006, amended by RD 732/2019 and RD 450/2022) | Technical requirements: DB SE, SI, SUA, HE, HR, HS |
| Structural Code (Código Estructural, RD 470/2021) | Concrete and steel |
| NCSE-02 (RD 997/2002) | Earthquakes. Especially in Granada, Murcia, Alicante, Málaga, Almería |
| RD 1000/2010 | **Compulsory** endorsement by the professional body (*visado*) of the construction design of a building (and of the final completion certificate) |
| RD 1627/1997 | Health and safety study or basic study |
| RD 105/2008 | Waste management study within the design |
| RD 390/2021 | Energy efficiency certificate of the design |
| Regional accessibility and habitability rules | Minimum areas, accessibility, sometimes stricter than the CTE |

## Where to go

| Institution | What you ask for | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Official Association of Architects** (*Colegio Oficial de Arquitectos*, Annex A) | List of architects, endorsement of the design | *Visado* (checks identity, qualification, insurance and that the design is complete) |
| **Association of Technical Architects** (*Colegio de Aparejadores y Arquitectos Técnicos*, Annex A) | Director of works execution and health and safety coordinator | Registered professionals and endorsement of their work |
| **Geotechnical laboratory** entered in the CCAA's register | Geotechnical study (CTE DB SE-C) | Type of foundation, water table, allowable bearing pressures |
| **OCT** | Review of the design | D0 report for the ten-year insurer |
| **Ten-year insurer** (through a broker) | Pre-review of the risk | Insurance terms before the design is closed |

## Lesson 3.1 — The LOE and the technical team

> **Where we are:** with the GO from Module 2 and the plot bought, you start the technical designs. First, the team: what each party does under the LOE and how you hire each professional.

| Party | LOE article | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Developer | 9 | Decides, finances, commissions. Takes out the insurance. Jointly liable with the other parties towards the buyers (art. 17.3) |
| Designer | 10 | Writes the design. For housing: an architect |
| Contractor | 11 | Builds according to the design and the instructions of the site supervision team |
| Works director | 12 | Directs the technical development. For housing: an architect |
| Director of works execution | 13 | Controls the physical execution and quality. For housing: a technical architect |
| Quality control bodies and laboratories | 14 | Tests and technical assistance |
| Product suppliers | 15 | Manufacturers and distributors |
| Owners and users | 16 | Maintain and use the building according to the manual |

- **Hire each professional in writing:** scope, fees, deadlines, minimum site visits, professional liability (RC) insurance.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistake:** buying a "cheap" design and then paying the difference in variations during the works.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the plot in his SPE's name, Nicolás put together his technical team. Let me tell you how.

**He is the developer.** He is first in the table: he decides, finances, commissions and takes out the insurance, and he is jointly liable with the other parties towards the buyers. That is why he chose each professional calmly and hired them all in writing.

**The architect.** For housing, the designer and the works director have to be architects. He stayed with the architecture practice in his area that did his massing study (Lesson 1.2) and his fit study (Lesson 2.2): it does the design and the works direction. The structural and building services engineering is coordinated by the architect. You will find the architecture practices and engineering firms in your province in the list we have given you: among the potential companies for this lesson.

**The technical architect and the coordinator.** For the direction of works execution, which on housing is done by a technical architect, and for the health and safety coordination, he went to the **Association of Technical Architects** in his province, which gave him registered professionals ("Where to go" table). You have the one in your province at the end of the module.

**The contracts.** He signed one with each professional (the architect, the technical architect, the health and safety coordinator and each engineering firm) with what the lesson asks for:

- the scope of the work;
- the fees, which he compared with the item in his CRP (8 % of construction: 83.766,67 €, Lesson 2.3);
- the deadlines;
- the minimum site visits;
- each one's professional liability (RC) insurance.

**What he did not do.** He did not choose the cheapest: a cheap design is paid for later in variations during the works. The geotechnical laboratory and the OCT come in Lessons 3.2 and 3.3.

### Summary

The LOE says what each party does and who is liable. As the developer you choose each professional (on housing, an architect for the design and the works direction, a technical architect for the direction of works execution) and hire them in writing: scope, fees, deadlines, visits and insurance.

### Your checklist

- Contracts signed with the architect, technical architect, CSS and engineers

## Lesson 3.2 — The Building Code (CTE)

> **Where we are:** you now have the team hired (Lesson 3.1). Now you go over the technical requirements your design has to meet: the Building Code (CTE) and the compulsory rules that sit outside it.

| Basic document | What it governs |
|---|---|
| DB SE (+ SE-AE, SE-C, SE-A, SE-F, SE-M) | Structural safety, actions, foundations, steel, masonry, timber |
| DB SI | Fire: evacuation, fire resistance, installations |
| DB SUA | Safety in use and accessibility. SUA 9: an accessible lift when more than two storeys must be climbed from the accessible entrance or there are more than 12 homes on storeys without an accessible entrance |
| DB HE | Energy: HE0 consumption, HE1 envelope, HE2 thermal installations (RITE), HE3 lighting, HE4 renewables for domestic hot water (ACS), HE5 minimum renewable power generation in large new buildings (threshold of 1.000 m² built; check with your designer whether your building falls in), HE6 electric vehicle charging infrastructure |
| DB HR | Noise |
| DB HS | Health and hygiene: damp, waste, air quality, water, drainage, radon (HS6) |

- **Outside the CTE but compulsory:** Structural Code, NCSE-02 (earthquakes), REBT, RITE, ICT, RIPCI.
- **Radon (HS6):** compulsory in the municipalities listed in appendix B of DB HS. Check it before designing the basement.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the team hired, Nicolás went over with his architect what the CTE and the rules outside it require of his building. Let me tell you what applied to him.

**The ground, first.** The foundations are calculated with DB SE-C, and for that you need the geotechnical study. Nicolás commissioned it from a **geotechnical laboratory entered in the Comunitat Valenciana's register** ("Where to go" table), before the outline design (Lesson 3.3). The laboratory gives him the type of foundation, the water table and the allowable bearing pressures. You have the laboratories in your province at the end of the module.

**Earthquakes.** Alicante is one of the provinces the module's table points to for NCSE-02, the earthquake-resistance standard. It is not in the CTE, but it is compulsory: the architect and the structural engineers include it in the calculation, together with the Structural Code.

**The lift.** His building is B+5: from the entrance more than two storeys must be climbed, so DB SUA (SUA 9) requires an accessible lift. It was already in the fit study (Lesson 2.2).

**Energy.** DB HE applies in full: envelope, thermal installations, lighting, renewables for hot water and electric vehicle charging. For HE5, the lesson gives a threshold of 1.000 m² built and his building has 1.586,49 m²: he asked the architect to confirm whether it falls in, as the lesson advises.

**Radon.** It has no basement, but he checked with the architect whether his municipality is in appendix B of DB HS.

**The rest, outside the CTE.** The REBT, the RITE, the ICT and the RIPCI are handled by each engineering firm in its part, coordinated by the architect. And the regional accessibility and habitability rules, which sometimes ask for more than the CTE on minimum areas and accessibility.

### Summary

The CTE sets in its basic documents what the building must meet (structure, fire, accessibility, energy, noise and health), and there are rules outside it that are also compulsory. Go over them with your architect before designing: the ground, earthquakes, the lift, energy and radon.

### Your checklist

- Geotechnical study

## Lesson 3.3 — Design stages

> **Where we are:** you have the team (Lesson 3.1) and you know what the CTE requires of your building (Lesson 3.2). Now the design moves forward in stages: outline design, basic design to apply for the licence and construction design to build.

1. **Preliminary study / outline design** (2-4 weeks): massing, unit types, number of homes. Not endorsed. Used to settle the mix with the sales team.
2. **Basic design** (4-8 weeks): defines the building and shows compliance with the planning rules and the basic requirements of the CTE. Most municipalities grant the licence on it (some ask for the construction design). Endorsing the basic design is voluntary unless the town hall requires it.
3. **Construction design** (a further 6-12 weeks): full construction detail. **Compulsory endorsement** (RD 1000/2010). It is what you contract with the builder and what you need to start the works.
- **Contents of the construction design:**
  - Descriptive and construction report, and report of compliance with the CTE
  - Drawings: plans, elevations, sections, structure, building services, details
  - Specifications
  - Measurements and budget (PEM)
  - Geotechnical study (appendix)
  - Waste management study (RD 105/2008)
  - Health and safety study or basic study (RD 1627/1997)
  - ICT design (RD 346/2011), signed by a telecommunications engineer or qualified professional
  - Energy efficiency certificate of the design (RD 390/2021)
  - Quality control plan
- **Who handles what** (who prepares each document in the list above):
  - **Architect (designer):** the descriptive and construction report and the report of compliance with the CTE; the plans, elevations, sections and details; the specifications; the measurements and the budget (PEM); the waste management study and the quality control plan.
  - **Engineering firms** (coordinated by the architect): the structure and building services drawings.
  - **Telecommunications engineer or qualified professional:** the ICT design.
  - **Architect or building services engineering firm:** the energy efficiency certificate of the design.
  - **Geotechnical laboratory:** the geotechnical study, which goes in as an appendix.
  - **Qualified professional appointed by the developer** (Lesson 3.4): the health and safety study or basic study.
  - **You (developer):** you commission and pay for each one, and you appoint the health and safety professional in writing.
- **Tip:** before endorsement, ask the OCT and two contractors to review the design. Every mistake you find there costs ten times less than on site.

- **Surveys and technical studies before the outline design:**
  - **Georeferenced** topographic survey (it also helps you align Cadastre and Land Registry, Ley 13/2015).
  - Geotechnical study of the ground.
  - Investigation of contamination, made ground or buried services where relevant.
  - Inspection of existing buildings and demolition plan.
  - Enquiries to the utility companies about water, sewerage, electricity, gas and telecoms networks.
  - Access, levels, drainage and conditions of the surroundings.
- **Brief:** number of homes and areas by unit type, orientation, views, privacy, energy efficiency, garages, storerooms, lifts, courtyards and common areas.
- **Design to target cost:** fit the design to the sale prices and to the cost in the CRP before moving on to the basic design.
- **At basic design stage,** ask the sectoral bodies for their preliminary reports where needed.
- **At construction design stage,** coordinate architecture, structure, electrical, HVAC, plumbing and drainage (with BIM if you can) and prepare measurements, budget and tender documents.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the geotechnical study done, Nicolás's architect got going on the design. Let me tell you how each stage went.

**Before drawing.** Nicolás asked the surveyor from Lesson 1.3 for the **georeferenced** topographic survey, which also helps him align Cadastre and Land Registry, and asked the water, sewerage, electricity, gas and telecoms companies about their networks. There were no buildings to demolish (Lesson 1.1).

**The outline design.** He gave the architect his brief: 18 homes with 2 and 3 bedrooms, 2 of them penthouses, a mid-range specification, a lift and no basement. Within a few weeks (the lesson talks of 2-4) he had the outline design, which is not endorsed, and he validated it against his sales mix from Lesson 2.2. Before moving on to the basic design he fitted the design to his target cost: the 1.259,70 €/m² sale price and the construction in his CRP.

**The basic design.** It defines the building and shows compliance with the planning rules and the CTE: it is the one he will apply for the licence with (Module 4). He asked at the planning department whether his town hall requires it to be endorsed, because if not, it is voluntary. His plot has no easements (Lesson 1.3), so he did not need preliminary reports from sectoral bodies.

**The construction design.** The architect coordinated architecture, structure, electrical, HVAC, plumbing and drainage with the engineering firms, and prepared the measurements, the budget and the documents to tender the works (Module 5). You will find the engineering firms in your province in the list we have given you, among the potential companies for this lesson.

**Before endorsement, the review.** He followed the lesson's tip:

- The **OCT** reviewed the design and issued the **D0 report** for the ten-year insurer.
- Through an **insurance broker**, the **ten-year insurer** did the pre-review of the risk and gave him the insurance terms before the design was closed.
- Two contractors went over it too: every mistake found here costs ten times less than on site.

You have the OCTs in your province at the end of the module.

### Summary

The design moves forward in stages: the outline design settles the mix, the basic design is used to apply for the licence and the construction design, with compulsory endorsement, to contract and build. Before endorsement, have it reviewed by the OCT, the ten-year insurer and two contractors.

### Your checklist

- Preliminary design validated against the sales mix
- Basic design (endorsed by the professional body if the town hall requires it)
- Design review by the OCT (D0 report)
- Pre-review by the ten-year insurer

## Lesson 3.4 — Health and safety in the design

> **Where we are:** the construction design is almost ready (Lesson 3.3). It is still missing health and safety: which document your works need, who writes it and who coordinates.

| Document | When | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Health and safety study** | PEM ≥ 450.759,08 €, or more than 30 working days with more than 20 workers at the same time, or more than 500 worker-days, or tunnels, galleries, dams | A qualified professional appointed by the developer |
| **Basic health and safety study** | All other works | A qualified professional |
| **Health and safety plan** | Always, before starting | Each contractor. Approved by the coordinator during the works (or by the site supervision team if there is no coordinator) |

- **Health and safety coordinator:**
  - At design stage: compulsory if several designers take part.
  - During the works: compulsory if more than one company takes part, or a company and self-employed workers, or several self-employed workers. On a development, always.
  - Appointed by the developer. Keeps the **incidents book**.
- **Liability:** a serious accident can end in criminal liability (arts. 316-318 of the Criminal Code). Appoint the coordinator in writing and check that they visit the site.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Nicolás's construction design was still missing health and safety. Let me tell you how he sorted it out.

**Study or basic study?** The table requires the full study from a PEM of 450.759,08 €, and his works are well above that: the construction in his CRP stands at 1.047.083,40 € (Lesson 2.3). He needs the **health and safety study**, not the basic one.

**Who writes it.** A qualified professional appointed by the developer. Nicolás appointed in writing the health and safety coordinator he hired through the Association of Technical Architects (Lesson 3.1). At design stage one was already required, because several designers take part: the architect, the engineering firms and the ICT professional.

**The coordinator on site.** During the works it is compulsory too: with the contractor, subcontractors and self-employed workers will come in, and on a development there is always a coordinator. Nicolás appoints them, in writing. They will keep the **incidents book**, which is obtained from the professional association at the start (Module 4).

**The plan, once there is a contractor.** Each contractor will draw up its health and safety plan before starting, and the coordinator during the works will approve it. It comes with the construction contract (Module 5).

**Liability.** A serious accident can end in criminal liability. That is why he put the appointment in writing and included the coordinator's minimum site visits in their contract (Lesson 3.1): that way he can check that they go.

**Endorsement.** With the health and safety study included, the architect took the construction design to the **Official Association of Architects**, which checks identity, qualification, insurance and that the design is complete. It contains everything on the list in Lesson 3.3: the geotechnical study, waste management, the ICT and the energy efficiency certificate.

**Where he is now.** He has the basic design to apply for the licence and the construction design endorsed, with the OCT's D0 report and the ten-year insurer's terms. He moves on to Module 4: the building licence.

### Summary

Depending on the size of the works, your design includes a health and safety study or a basic study, and each contractor will draw up its plan before starting. You appoint the coordinator yourself, in writing: on a development, there is always one during the works.

### Your checklist

- Construction design endorsed, with ESS, waste management, ICT and energy efficiency
