# Licences and administrative authorisations

> **Objective:** get the building licence in the shortest possible time and open the site without breaching anything.
> **Duration:** 100 min · **Result:** licence granted, ICIO paid, workplace opening notified and setting-out record signed.
> **Where we are:** with the design from Module 3, you apply for the building licence and get the works ready to start without breaching anything. The sectoral authorisations from other bodies (Lesson 4.2) are part of the licence documentation (Lesson 4.1).

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| TRLSRU (RDL 7/2015), art. 11.4 | New-build works need an express decision. **No decision, no licence: silence is negative** |
| Regional planning law (Annex B) | Type of permit (licence or responsible declaration), documents, deadlines to decide and expiry |
| Ley 39/2015, on the Common Administrative Procedure | Correction within 10 days (art. 68), appeals (reconsideration: 1 month; judicial review: 2 months) |
| RDL 2/2004 (TRLRHL), arts. 100-103 | ICIO: up to 4 %, due when the works start; provisional and final assessment on the actual cost |
| Ley 21/2013, on environmental assessment | Urban projects above the thresholds of annex II, or those set by the CCAA |
| RD 849/1986 (Public Water Domain) | A 100 m control zone on each side of a watercourse |
| Ley 22/1988, on Coasts | Protection easement (100 m; 20 m on land that was urban in 1988) and right-of-way easement |
| Ley 37/2015 (roads) and Ley 38/2015 (railways) | Protection zones and building line |
| Ley 48/1960 and RD 297/2013 (aeronautical easements) | Maximum heights near airports: AESA report |
| Ley 16/1985 + regional heritage law | Listed assets of cultural interest and archaeological zones |
| RD 1627/1997, art. 19 + Orden TIN/1071/2010 | Notification of the opening of the workplace |
| Ley 32/2006 + RD 1109/2007 | Register of Accredited Companies (REA) and subcontracting book |

## Lesson 4.1 — The building licence

> **Where we are:** with the design from Module 3, you apply to the town hall for the building licence: first you confirm which permit applies to you and then you file the complete application. Procedures, fees and deadlines vary by municipality and autonomous community: check them with your administrative agent (*gestor*) or your lawyer.

- **First, the type of control:** confirm which permit applies under the regional law and the by-law (licence, responsible declaration or prior notification) and whether you need **separate licences** for demolition, earthworks, urbanisation works or tree felling.

- **Which permit you need:** for new residential building, a **major works licence** (*licencia de obras mayor*). Some regional laws allow a responsible declaration in specific cases; for new build the rule is still an express licence (art. 11.4 TRLSRU).
- **Step by step:**
  1. Preliminary meeting with the municipal officer, with the outline design. Ask which sectoral reports they will require.
  2. File through the e-office: application, design (basic or construction, depending on the town hall), health and safety study, waste management study, any sectoral authorisations you already have, proof of payment of fees and ICIO if the by-law requires it on application, waste deposit if the by-law asks for one.
     - **Sectoral authorisations (Lesson 4.2):** these are the permits from other bodies (river basin authority, Coasts, roads, AESA, heritage) when the plot touches a protection zone. They form part of the documentation for this building licence.
  3. The town hall checks that the file is complete. If something is missing it sends you a request, and you have **10 days** to correct it.
  4. Technical report (municipal architect) and legal report.
  5. Decision. If favourable, the licence sets the start and completion deadlines and conditions.
  6. If refused: appeal for reconsideration (1 month, optional) or judicial review (2 months).
- **Why it takes so long and how to shorten it:** the administration's slowness often comes from poorly conceived designs, with deficiencies due to regulatory or structural non-compliance or non-compliance with the Building Code (CTE): a reliable architecture team saves you months. In the Comunitat Valenciana, many town halls have an agreement with **urban planning collaborating entities (ECUV)** (*entidades colaboradoras urbanísticas*), which review the design within short deadlines: when it reaches the town hall already reviewed by an ECUV, the town hall is spared that check. With an ECUV the licence can come through in about three months, depending on the design (longer if, for example, a flood-risk problem comes up).
- **Administrative silence:** on new build **it is negative** (art. 11.4 TRLSRU). If the deadline passes with no decision, you cannot start.
- **ICIO:**
  - Base: the actual cost of the physical execution. It does not include IVA, fees, professional fees or the contractor's profit (art. 102.1 TRLRHL).
  - Rate: the by-law's, 4 % at most. Possible rebates (up to 95 %) for works of special interest, solar energy, accessibility or protected housing, if the by-law provides for them.
  - It falls due when the works start. Provisional assessment when the licence is applied for or granted, and final assessment at completion, on the actual cost.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** filing an incomplete design (every request adds weeks); not asking about sectoral reports beforehand; starting on silence, thinking it is positive.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the design from Module 3, Nicolás went after the licence. Let me tell you how he did it.

**First, which permit he needed.** He confirmed it with his planning lawyer, the one from the due diligence (Lesson 1.3): for his new building, B+5 with 18 homes, a **major works licence**, with an express decision. There was nothing to demolish (Lesson 1.1) and nothing to urbanise (Lesson 1.2); he still had to find out whether his town hall required a separate licence for earthworks or tree felling. You will find the law firms and architecture practices in your province in the list we have given you: among the potential companies for this lesson.

**The preliminary meeting.** Before filing anything he went with his architect to the **town hall's planning department**, to see the municipal officer with the outline design. He took three questions: which sectoral reports they would ask him for (Lesson 4.2), which design the licence is applied for with in his municipality (the basic or the construction design) and whether any separate licence was needed. Whatever they asked for separately he would file together with the building licence.

**The application, complete.** He filed it through the **town hall's e-office** with everything in the lesson: the application, the design, the health and safety study (not the basic one: his works exceed the 450.759,08 € of Lesson 3.4), the waste management study and what his by-law requires on application: proof of payment of the fees and ICIO and the waste deposit. He did not need any sectoral authorisations (Lesson 4.2).

**The ICIO.** He paid it at the **town hall's tax office**: his by-law's rate (4 % at most) on the actual cost of the physical execution, without IVA, fees, professional fees or the contractor's profit. In Template 2 he had set aside 111.054,30 € for licences, ICIO and fees. The final assessment will come at completion, on the actual cost: that is why he keeps every invoice from the works.

**Then, waiting without moving.** If the town hall had asked him for something, he would have had 10 days to correct it; that is why he filed it complete: every request adds weeks. The technical report from the municipal architect and the legal report came in (the "Where to go" table talks of 1-6 months depending on the municipality), and in the meantime not a single machine entered the plot: on new build, silence is negative. The decision came back favourable, with its deadlines and conditions (Lesson 4.4).

### Summary

Confirm which permit you need and whether separate licences are required, meet the municipal officer beforehand and file the complete application, with the fees and the ICIO. On new build, silence is negative: without an express decision you do not start.

### Your checklist

- Preliminary meeting with the planning department
- Complete application (design, ESS, waste, fees, deposits)
- ICIO paid
- Demolition, earthworks or tree-felling licences if they apply

## Lesson 4.2 — Sectoral authorisations

> **Where we are:** in Lesson 4.1 you prepared the building licence application. Now you check which sectoral authorisations from other bodies you need, which form part of that application, and you prepare the complementary steps so you can work.

- **How to know which ones you need:** check in the PGOU viewer and the sectoral viewers (river basin authority, Coasts, roads, AESA, heritage) whether the plot touches any protection zone. Apply in parallel: do not wait for the town hall to ask for them.

| If the plot is… | You need |
|---|---|
| Less than 100 m from a river or ravine | Authorisation from the river basin authority or regional water agency |
| In the coastal protection strip | Authorisation from the CCAA and a Coasts report |
| Next to a road | Authorisation from its owner (State, CCAA or *Diputación*) |
| Next to a railway line | Authorisation from ADIF |
| Near an airport (heights) | AESA report |
| In an archaeological zone or with a BIC | Report or archaeological intervention by the culture department |
| Above environmental thresholds | Environmental impact assessment (the CCAA's environmental body) |
| Near a military installation | Authorisation from the Ministry of Defence |

- **Complementary steps so you can work:**
  - **Utility connections:** supply and connection-point applications to the electricity distributor, the water company and the other companies.
  - **Use of the public highway:** hoardings, scaffolding, skips and storage pay a municipal fee.
  - **Tower crane:** installation project, erection by an authorised company and registration with the community's Industry Service (ITC MIE-AEM 2, RD 836/2003). Some town halls also require authorisation for swinging over the public highway.
  - **Road closures** for concrete pours or deliveries: municipal authorisation and local police.
  - **Outstanding urbanisation works:** approved urbanisation project; many town halls require a guarantee if you urbanise while you build.
  - Authorisations for the installations that need them.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the application under way, Nicolás checked which permits from other bodies he needed and prepared what is needed to work. Let me tell you how.

**First, the protection zones.** He checked in the PGOU viewer and the sectoral viewers whether his plot touched any zone in the table: a river or ravine less than 100 m away, the coast, a road, a railway line, an airport's heights, an archaeological zone or a BIC. You have the websites of the river basin authority, Coasts, ADIF and AESA among this lesson's websites. His architect had already reviewed the easements in the due diligence (Lesson 1.3), and at the preliminary meeting he asked the municipal officer which sectoral reports they would ask him for (Lesson 4.1).

**The result.** His plot does not touch any protection zone: he does not need any sectoral authorisations. If he had needed one, he would have applied for it in parallel, without waiting for the town hall to ask for it: some take 2 to 6 months ("Where to go" table). In Alicante, the river basin authority is the Júcar's (*Confederación Hidrográfica del Júcar*) or, in the south, the Segura's; you will find the bodies in your province at the end of the module.

**Then, what is needed to work.** With no permits from other bodies, he still had the complementary steps:

- **Utility connections:** the supply and connection-point applications to the electricity distributor, the water company and the other companies. He already knew there was capacity (Lesson 1.3); he applied early, because connection lead times are long (Module 6).
- **Use of the public highway:** hoardings, scaffolding, skips and storage, with their municipal fee, at the town hall.
- **Tower crane:** the installation project, erection by an authorised company and registration with his community's Industry Service. He also asked whether his town hall requires authorisation for swinging over the public highway.
- **Road closures** for concrete pours and deliveries: municipal authorisation and local police.

He settled the public highway, the crane and the road closures with the contractor once he had hired it (Lessons 5.3 and 5.4): it is the contractor that knows which crane it puts up, where the skips go and on which days it pours concrete. He had no outstanding urbanisation works: his plot already has services.

### Summary

Check in the viewers whether your plot touches any protection zone and apply in parallel for the authorisations you need: they form part of the licence. And prepare what is needed to work: utility connections, public highway, crane and road closures.

### Your checklist

- Sectoral authorisations identified and applied for in parallel
- Use of the public highway, crane and road closures authorised

## Lesson 4.3 — Starting the works

> **Where we are:** you have the licence granted (Lessons 4.1 and 4.2). Now you prepare the start of the works: what must be done, and in what order, before the first lorry comes in, so that you open the site without breaching anything.

- **Order of steps before the first lorry:**
  1. Licence granted and its conditions read (deposits, site board, deadlines).
  2. **Health and safety coordinator** appointed in writing by the developer.
  3. The contractor's **health and safety plan** approved by the coordinator.
  4. **Notification of the opening of the workplace** to the CCAA's labour authority: filed by the contractor, with the health and safety plan, before starting.
  5. Contractor and subcontractors entered in the **REA**.
  6. **Subcontracting book** validated by the labour authority.
  7. **Orders and attendance book** (site supervision team) and **incidents book** (coordinator).
  8. **Setting-out verification record** (*acta de comprobación del replanteo*) signed by the works director, the director of works execution and the contractor. It sets the start date of the construction period.
  9. **Site board** as the by-law requires (licence number, developer, professionals, contractor, deadlines).
  10. Notification of the start to the town hall if the by-law asks for it.
- **Who handles what** (who does each step in the order above):
  - **You, the developer:** you read the licence and its conditions (deposits, site board, deadlines), appoint the health and safety coordinator in writing, make sure the site board is put up and notify the start to the town hall if the by-law asks for it.
  - **Contractor (the builder):** writes its health and safety plan, files the notification of the opening of the workplace with that plan, is entered in the REA with its subcontractors, has the subcontracting book validated and signs the setting-out verification record.
  - **Health and safety coordinator:** approves the contractor's health and safety plan and keeps the incidents book.
  - **Site supervision team** (works director and director of works execution): keeps the orders and attendance book and signs, with the contractor, the setting-out verification record.
  - **CCAA's labour authority:** receives the opening notification, keeps the REA and validates the subcontracting book.
  - **Association of Architects / of Technical Architects:** the orders and attendance book and the incidents book, at the start.
  - **Town hall:** grants the licence with its conditions and says in its by-law what the site board must show and whether it asks for the notification of the start.
- **Deed declaring the new building and the horizontal division** (Module 9), unless it is a detached single-family house and only the new building is declared because there are no separate units to divide.
  - The developer may, where appropriate, obtain external financing, that is, a developer loan to carry out the development. The documents the lender will ask for concern the developer's solvency and repayment guarantees, as well as the project to be developed.
  - As for the mortgage loan to be granted to the buyer, the lender will not grant any mortgage until the Final Completion Deed is registered at the Land Registry; that is, while the "under construction" note is still registered, no mortgage is signed on the purchase.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistake:** starting the excavation before the opening notification. It is a serious breach of health and safety rules.

- **Organise the site supervision team before starting:** appoint in writing the architect as works director, the technical architect as director of works execution, the health and safety coordinator, the specialist professionals and the quality control, with the scope of each.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the licence granted, Nicolás prepared the start of the works. Let me tell you in the lesson's order, although he settled several steps with the contractor already hired (Lessons 5.3 and 5.4): without a contractor there is no safety plan, no opening notification and no setting-out record.

**First, the site supervision team, in writing.** He appointed in writing, with the scope of each, the architect as works director (the practice that did the design), the technical architect as director of works execution and the health and safety coordinator (hired in Module 3), the specialist professionals from the engineering firms and the quality control.

**The safety plan and the opening.** The contractor wrote its health and safety plan and the coordinator approved it. With the plan approved, the contractor filed the **notification of the opening of the workplace** with the community's **labour authority**: in the Comunitat Valenciana, the INVASSAT. You will find the one in your province at the end of the module.

**The companies, in the REA.** Nicolás checked, in the **REA** public search (you have it among this lesson's websites), that the contractor and each subcontractor were entered, and that the contractor had its subcontracting book validated.

**The books.** At the start, the site supervision team opened the orders and attendance book, and the coordinator the incidents book: they are issued by the Association of Architects and the Association of Technical Architects ("Where to go" table).

**The setting-out record.** It was signed by the works director, the director of works execution and the contractor. The construction period runs from that date: in his Template 3, 20 months (Lesson 5.1).

**The site board and the town hall.** He put up the site board with what the by-law requires (licence number, developer, professionals, contractor, deadlines) and notified the start to the town hall, at the same time as to the bank, the ten-year insurer and the OCT (Lesson 5.4).

**The deed.** His building has 18 homes: there are units to divide. At the beginning of the works he signed, at his notary's, the **deed declaring the new building and the horizontal division**, with the licence and the professional's certificate that the description matches the design, and the **Land Registry** registered it as "under construction" (Lessons 9.2 and 10.3).

**What he did not do.** Not a single machine entered the plot before the opening notification: starting the excavation before that is a serious breach of health and safety rules.

### Summary

Before the first lorry, in order: licence read, coordinator appointed, safety plan approved, opening notification, REA, books, setting-out record and site board. And the site supervision team, appointed in writing before starting.

### Your checklist

- Coordinator appointed and safety plan approved
- Opening notification, REA and subcontracting book
- Orders and incidents books
- Setting-out record signed and site board put up

## Lesson 4.4 — Deadlines, extensions and expiry

> **Where we are:** you have the licence granted and you know what is needed to open the site (Lesson 4.3). What remains is to control its deadlines: the start deadline, the completion deadline, the extension and expiry.

| Item | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| Start deadline | The one set by the licence or the regional law (usually 6 months to 1 year) |
| Completion deadline | The licence's (usually 2-3 years) |
| Extension | Ask for it in writing **before** it runs out. It is usually granted once |
| Expiry | It is not automatic: the town hall opens a procedure in which you are heard. Once declared, you need a new licence |

- **Tip:** apply for the licence when your financing is on track. A licence that expires in a falling market is an enormous cost.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the licence in his hand, Nicolás read its conditions slowly. Let me tell you how he keeps track of the deadlines.

**The conditions, on day one.** He reviewed what the licence asked of him: the deposits, the site board and, above all, the deadlines. The licence sets the start deadline and the completion deadline (usually 6 months to 1 year to start and 2 to 3 years to finish). He put them in his calendar that same day. The start deadline runs while he signs the loan and hires the contractor (Module 5); the completion deadline, while the works last: in his Template 3, 20 months of works (Lesson 5.1).

**The extension, before it runs out.** If he sees he will not meet a deadline, he will ask for the extension in writing before it runs out. He knows it is usually granted once.

**Expiry.** It is not automatic: the town hall opens a procedure in which he is heard. But, if it is declared, he needs a new licence and goes back to the beginning of Lesson 4.1.

**The tip, followed.** He applied for the licence with his financing on track: before filing the application he had already talked to his bank about the development loan, which he closes in Module 5. A licence that expires in a falling market is an enormous cost.

### Summary

The licence sets the start and completion deadlines. You ask for the extension in writing before it runs out, and expiry is not automatic, but once declared it forces you to apply for a new licence: apply for the licence with your financing on track.

### Your checklist

- Licence granted and its conditions reviewed
