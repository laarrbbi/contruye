# Handover of the keys

> **Objective:** sign, get paid, set up the owners' association and hand over homes that can be lived in from day one.
> **Duration:** 115 min · **Result:** deeds signed, development loan repaid, owners' association set up and handover records.
> **Where we are:** with the homes checked and the notary ready (Module 10), the signing comes: deeds, payment, owners' association, utilities and handing over the keys.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| Civil Code (*Código Civil*), arts. 1445 et seq., 1455 | Sale and the legal split of the deed costs |
| TRLGDCU, art. 89.3 | Costs and taxes you cannot pass on |
| Ley 5/2019, art. 14 | Mortgage costs: the bank pays the notary, registry and agency costs of the mortgage; the buyer pays the valuation and their copies |
| RDL 1/1993 (ITP-AJD) + RD-ley 17/2018 | The bank pays the AJD on the mortgage. The buyer pays the AJD on the purchase of a new home |
| LPH (Ley 49/1960) | Owners' association, general meeting, reserve fund (at least 10 % of the ordinary budget, art. 9.1.f, since Ley 8/2013) |
| TRLRHL, arts. 104-110 | Municipal plusvalía: the developer pays it as the seller |
| Ley 10/2010 and Ley 7/2012 | Identification of payments and the cash limit |

## Where to go

| Institution | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Notary** | Signing of the sale and, where relevant, the buyer's mortgage or a partial cancellation |
| **Land Registry** | Registration in the buyer's name (handled by the notary or the agency) |
| **AEAT** | NIF of the owners' association (form 036) |
| **Bank** | The owners' association's account |
| **Distributor and retailers** | Electricity contracts for each home (with the CIE and first occupation or cédula) |
| **Water company** | Water contracts for each home |
| **Town hall** | Municipal plusvalía on each sale (you pay it if the land has increased in value; if not, it is advisable to declare it) and change of IBI taxpayer |
| **Association of Property Managers** | A property manager for the owners' association |

## Lesson 11.1 — The deed of sale

> **Where we are:** with the homes snagged and the notary prepared (Module 10), signing day arrives. You start with the deed of sale: what it contains, who pays what and what happens to the development loan.

- **Contents:** parties, registered property and description, price and payment terms (with earlier payments identified), encumbrances (free of encumbrances, or with the take-over chosen by the buyer), statements (ten-year insurance, licences, energy certificate, cédula), costs.
- At the signing of the sale at the notary's, the home buyer is given the **Building Book** (Lesson 9.4).
- **Who pays what:**

| Cost | Buyer | Developer |
|---|---|---|
| IVA 10 % (IGIC in Canarias, IPSI in Ceuta and Melilla) | ✔ (you charge it) | — |
| AJD on the sale (Annex B) | ✔ | — |
| Notary for the sale | Their copy | The original deed (art. 1455 CC, unless agreed otherwise; with a consumer, do not impose costs that are yours) |
| Registration of their purchase | ✔ | — |
| The buyer's mortgage | Valuation and copies | The bank pays AJD, notary, registry and agency |
| Municipal plusvalía | — | ✔ (cannot be passed on) |
| Cancellation of the developer's mortgage | — | ✔ |
| New building and horizontal division | — | ✔ |
| General utility connections | — | ✔ |

- **Payment:** named banker's draft or a transfer confirmed at the notary's. If the buyer takes over the loan, their share becomes their mortgage and the bank deducts that amount.
- **Development loan:** with each deed, that property's share is cancelled or taken over. When the last home is sold, it must be at zero.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the Final Completion Deed registered and the calendar closed, the signings began. Let me tell you how one went, because all 18 were done the same way.

**At the notary's.** The deed of sale set out what the lesson says: the parties, the registered property and its description, the price and payment terms, with the earlier payments identified, the encumbrances, the statements (ten-year insurance, licences and energy certificate) and the costs. Nicolás brought that buyer's settlement statement (Lesson 10.3), so that the earlier payments matched what he had already collected. And at the signing he gave them the **Building Book** (Lesson 9.4).

**Who pays what.** He followed the lesson's table without charging the buyer anything that falls to him: the "Laws that apply" table reminds him that there are costs and taxes he cannot pass on.

- **The buyer:** the IVA at 10 %, which Nicolás charges them; the AJD on the sale (Annex B); their copy of the deed; the registration of their purchase and, if they buy with a mortgage, the valuation and their copies.
- **Their bank:** the AJD, the notary, the registry and the agency for the mortgage.
- **Nicolás:** the original deed; the new building and the horizontal division; the general utility connections.

**The development loan.** With each deed, Nicolás's bank cancelled that home's share of the development loan with what Nicolás collected at the signing (Lesson 11.3). So, on signing the last of the 18, the loan of 2.153.510,70 € in his Template 3 was at zero, within the 4 months he had allowed until the sale deeds.

**Where he went.** To his notary and to the one each buyer chose. If a buyer asks you for a notary or a bank in your province, you have them among the potential companies for this lesson.

### Summary

The deed of sale sets out the parties, the property, the price with the earlier payments, the encumbrances, the statements and the costs, and with it the buyer receives the Building Book. Each party pays its own, according to the table. With each signing, that property's share of the development loan is cancelled or taken over, and with the last one it is at zero.

### Your checklist

- Share of the development loan cancelled or taken over at each signing

## Lesson 11.2 — The owners' association

> **Where we are:** with the first deed signed (Lesson 11.1), the owners' association comes into being. Now you set it up: meeting, budget and charges, reserve fund, NIF, bank account and maintenance contracts.

- **It comes into being** with the first sale: from then on there is more than one owner. While you still have unsold homes, you pay their service charges.
- **Steps:**
  1. Call the founding meeting (with an agenda).
  2. Election of the chair and, if decided, a secretary and a property manager.
  3. Approval of the ordinary budget and the charges by share.
  4. **Reserve fund** of at least 10 % of the ordinary budget.
  5. The owners' association's NIF from the AEAT, and a bank account.
  6. Minute book validated at the Land Registry.
  7. Maintenance contracts: lift, PCI, cleaning, landscaping, building insurance.
- **Property manager:** advisable from day one. Ask for a registered one.
- **Who handles what** (who does each step on the list above):
  - **You (the developer), as owner of the unsold homes:** you call the founding meeting, with its agenda, and pay the service charges of the homes you still have.
  - **The owners' meeting:** elects the chair and, if decided, a secretary and a property manager; approves the ordinary budget, the charges by share and the reserve fund.
  - **Property manager** (registered; Association of Property Managers): appointed by the meeting, if so decided, and runs the owners' association from that day on.
  - **AEAT:** the owners' association's NIF (form 036).
  - **Bank:** the owners' association's account.
  - **Land Registry:** the validation of the minute book.
  - **Maintenance companies and insurer:** the contracts for the lift, PCI, cleaning and landscaping, and the building insurance.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the first deed, the owners' association came into being: from that day on, the building had more than one owner. Let me tell you how Nicolás got it going.

**First, the property manager.** He did not wait: the lesson recommends one from day one. He looked for a registered **property manager**; you will find one in the list we have given you, among the potential companies for this lesson and, at the end of the module, through the Association of Property Managers of your province.

**The founding meeting.** Nicolás called it with its agenda. At it:

1. The owners elected the chair and appointed as property manager the one Nicolás had proposed.
2. They approved the ordinary budget and each home's charges by its share, the one in the horizontal division.
3. They funded the reserve fund with at least 10 % of the ordinary budget.

**The formalities.** With the property manager he obtained the owners' association's NIF from the **Tax Agency** (form 036), opened its account at the **bank** and took the minute book to be validated at the **Land Registry**.

**The maintenance.** The maintenance contracts for the lift, the fire protection and the cleaning were signed, and the building insurance. That way, the owners' association works from day one.

**His own charges.** While he still had homes left to convey by deed, Nicolás paid their service charges, like any other owner.

### Summary

The owners' association comes into being with the first sale. You set it up at a meeting (chair, budget, charges by share and a reserve fund of at least 10 %), give it a NIF, a bank account and a minute book, and contract its maintenance. While you still have unsold homes, you pay their service charges.

### Your checklist

- Founding meeting, NIF, bank account and reserve fund
- Maintenance contracts for the owners' association

## Lesson 11.3 — Utilities and final payment

> **Where we are:** the owners' association is up and running (Lesson 11.2). Now you get each home's utilities ready, so that it can be lived in from day one, and you collect what is outstanding at each deed.

| Utility | What you need | Who signs up |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | Registered CIE, CUPS for each home, first occupation or cédula | Each buyer, with their retailer (the developer can leave it ready) |
| Water | First occupation or cédula, individual meter | The buyer, with the municipal company |
| Gas (if any) | Installation certificate | The buyer |
| Telecoms | Certified ICT | The buyer, with their operator |

- **Watch out:** electricity contracts can be held up for weeks if the permanent connection or the transformer station are not legalised. Start with the distributor in Module 6.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the owners' association up and running, Nicolás had two things to close for each home: that it could be lived in from day one, and collecting what was outstanding. Let me tell you how he did it.

**The utilities, ready.** Each buyer signs the contracts, but the developer can leave them ready. Each home's folder (Lesson 10.2) included the registered CIE and the CUPS, for electricity, and the individual meter, for water, with the first occupation licence. With that, each buyer signed up for electricity with their retailer, water with the municipal company and telecoms with their operator, over the certified ICT.

**No delays with electricity.** The lesson warns: electricity contracts can be held up for weeks if the permanent connection or the transformer station are not legalised. Nicolás had confirmed the capacity with the companies before buying (Lesson 1.3) and had started with the distributor in Module 6. You have your province's electricity distributor and water service at the end of the module.

**The final payment.** At each deed, the buyer paid the amount outstanding on their settlement statement (Lesson 10.3), by named banker's draft or a transfer confirmed at the notary's (Lesson 11.1). For a home at the average price, according to his Template 5:

- Price with IVA: 252.705,83 €.
- Collected before handover (reservation, private contract and instalments): 50.541,17 €.
- Collected at the deed: **202.164,66 €**.

The buyers with a mortgage paid with their bank's money, at the same signing. With the 18 deeds collected in this way, the homes were paid for in full and all that was left was handing over the keys (Lesson 11.4).

### Summary

Each buyer contracts their utilities, but you leave them ready: CIE, CUPS, meter and first occupation. Start early with the distributor, because electricity contracts can be held up for weeks. At the deed you collect what is outstanding, by named banker's draft or a transfer confirmed at the notary's.

### Your checklist

- Deeds signed and each home paid for
- Utility contracts set up

## Lesson 11.4 — Handing over possession

> **Where we are:** with the deed signed and paid (Lessons 11.1 and 11.3), you hand over possession: final visit, handover record and a walk-through of the home.

- **On the day of signing or by appointment:**
  - Final visit with the buyer and check of the snags.
  - **Record of handover of possession** signed (date, condition, meter readings, keys and remotes handed over, documents).
  - Walk-through of the home: consumer unit, aerothermal heat pump, stopcocks, maintenance.
- **Meter readings** in the record, and a note of outstanding incidents with the commitment to resolve them.
- **Warranties:** the LOE's 1, 3 and 10-year periods run **from handover of the works without reservations, or from when the reservations are remedied** (art. 17.1 LOE), not from handover to each buyer. Explain it to them in writing.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Nicolás handed over each home on the day of signing, on leaving the notary's, or by appointment for the buyers who preferred it. Let me tell you what a handover was like.

**The final visit.** With the buyer, and with their pre-handover sheet from Lesson 10.1 in hand, they checked that the snags noted in the second round had been fixed.

**The record of handover of possession.** They both signed it, with:

- The date and the condition of the home.
- The electricity and water meter readings.
- The keys to the home and the entrance and the access cards handed over.
- The documents: the folder from Lesson 10.2.
- The outstanding incidents, if any were left, with the commitment to resolve them.

**The walk-through.** He showed them the consumer unit, the HVAC and the stopcocks, and the maintenance that falls to them, which they have in their use manual.

**The warranties, in writing.** He left it in writing that the 1, 3 and 10-year periods do not start on the day of their handover: they run from handover of the works without reservations, or from when the reservations are remedied (Lesson 6.5). That way nobody gets a surprise when after-sales comes round (Module 12).

### Summary

Possession is handed over with a final visit, a signed record (date, condition, meters, keys, documents and outstanding incidents) and a walk-through of the home. Put in writing that the warranties run from handover of the works, not from handover to the buyer.

### Your checklist

- Handover records signed

## Lesson 11.5 — Formalities after signing

> **Where we are:** the keys have been handed over (Lesson 11.4). What remains are the formalities after signing: taxes on the sale, Registry, Cadastre, utilities and documents.

- **Taxes on the sale:** the buyer pays the AJD on their community's form 600 within the regional deadline (usually 30 working days); many notaries handle it. You declare the IVA charged on your form 303.
- **Registry:** the notary sends an electronic copy to the Land Registry the same day; then the copy with the tax paid is filed and registered in the buyer's name.
- **Cadastre:** the notary and the registry report the change of owner; no declaration needs filing.
- **Municipal plusvalía (IIVTNU):** on the sale of each home, the developer is, as a rule, obliged to pay it, provided the land has increased in value. If the developer proves that there has been no increase, the tax is not due (although it is advisable to declare it to the competent body).
- **Utilities:** new contracts or changes of holder into the buyer's name.
- **Documents:** hand over what belongs to the buyer and to the owners' association's property manager.
- **Who handles what** (who does each formality on the list above):
  - **The buyer:** pays the AJD on their community's form 600, within the regional deadline (many notaries handle it), and puts the utilities in their name, with new contracts or changes of holder.
  - **Notary:** sends the electronic copy to the Land Registry on the day of signing.
  - **Land Registry:** with the copy and the tax paid, registers the home in the buyer's name.
  - **Notary and Registry:** report the change of owner to the Cadastre; no one files a declaration.
  - **You (the developer):** you declare the IVA charged on your form 303 and hand over the documents to the buyer and to the property manager. And the municipal plusvalía on each sale: you pay it if the land has increased in value; if it has not, it is not due, but it is advisable to declare it to the competent body.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the keys handed over, Nicolás was left with the formalities of each signing. Let me tell you how they were shared out.

**What the notary did.** On the day of each signing it sent the electronic copy to the **Land Registry**. Then the copy with the tax paid was filed and the Registry registered the home in the buyer's name. The change of owner in the Cadastre was reported by the notary and the Registry: no one had to file a declaration.

**What each buyer did.** They paid the AJD on their purchase on the Comunitat Valenciana's form 600, within the regional deadline; many notaries handle it. And they put the utilities in their name, on the contracts they had signed up for (Lesson 11.3).

**What Nicolás did:**

- He declared the IVA charged on each deed on his quarterly form 303, with the Tax Agency.
- The municipal plusvalía on each sale: with his advisory firm he checked whether the land had increased in value. If it had, he paid it; if not, the tax was not due, but he declared it to the competent body all the same.
- He handed over to each buyer and to the owners' association's property manager the documents that belonged to them.
- He checked, home by home, that the deed was registered and the utilities were in its buyer's name.

When the last of the 18 was registered, the development was sold, conveyed by deed and handed over. What was left was what comes afterwards: the warranties and the close (Module 12).

### Summary

After signing, the buyer pays the AJD and puts the utilities in their name; the notary sends the deed to the Registry, which registers it and, with the notary, notifies the Cadastre. You declare the IVA charged, hand over the documents and pay the municipal plusvalía on each sale if the land has increased in value (if it has not, it is not due, although it is advisable to declare it).

### Your checklist

- Deeds lodged at the Land Registry and utilities in each buyer's name
- Municipal plusvalía on each sale paid, or declared if there is no increase in value
