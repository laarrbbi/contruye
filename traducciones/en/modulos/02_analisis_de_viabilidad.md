# Feasibility study

> **Objective:** decide on the numbers whether the deal goes ahead, and at what land price.
> **Duration:** 130 min · **Result:** developer's profit and loss account (CRP) with three scenarios and a GO / NO GO decision.
> **Where we are:** with the plot secured in Module 1 (bought or under a purchase option), you decide on the numbers whether the development goes ahead and how much you can pay for the land: this is the feasibility analysis.

## Laws that apply

| Rule | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|
| Ley 37/1992 (IVA), art. 91 | IVA 10 % on the sale of homes and on construction work on homes; 4 % on protected housing under the special scheme |
| Ley 27/2014 (IS) + Ley 7/2024 | IS rates (see lesson 2.4) |
| RDL 2/2004 (TRLRHL), arts. 100-103 | ICIO up to 4 % of the actual cost of the works |
| Orden ECO/805/2003 | Mortgage valuation. The bank's valuers use it, residual method included |
| RD 1492/2011 | Valuation regulation of the Land Act (expropriations and planning valuations) |

## Where to go

| Institution | What you ask for | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Approved valuation company** (Banco de España register) | Valuation of the land and of the finished building ("finished building hypothesis") | The value the bank will use |
| **Bank** (Annex A, Phase 2) | A preliminary conversation about financing | Indicative terms and pre-sales requirements |
| **Registry Statistics** (*Estadística Registral*, registradores.org) and the **Notaries' Statistics Portal** | Prices recorded in deeds by area | Real prices, not asking prices |

| **Town hall** | Tax by-law for the ICIO and fees | The exact ICIO rate in the municipality |

## Lesson 2.1 — The developer's profit and loss account (CRP)

> **Where we are:** with the plot secured in Module 1, you start the feasibility analysis with its central tool, the developer's profit and loss account (CRP): the account with which you decide on the numbers whether the deal goes ahead.

- **Structure:**
```
  REVENUE (sale of homes, garages, storerooms, commercial units; without IVA)
− Land (price + non-recoverable taxes + notary + registry + due diligence)
− Construction (PEC without IVA)
− External works and utility connections
− Professional fees (design, site supervision, CSS, engineering, OCT)
− Licences, ICIO and fees
− Insurance (ten-year, TRC, RC, surety)
− Sales and marketing (agencies, marketing, show flat)
− Overheads and management costs
− Finance costs
= PROFIT BEFORE TAX
− Corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre Sociedades)
= NET PROFIT
```
- **Work without IVA** in the CRP: the IVA you pay is recovered when you charge 10 % on the sales.
- **Indicative weights as a share of revenue:**

| Item | % of revenue |
|---|---|
| Land | 15–30 % |
| Construction | 45–55 % |
| Professional fees | 5–8 % |
| Licences and fees | 2–4 % |
| Finance costs | 3–6 % |
| Sales and marketing | 2–4 % |
| Overheads | 3–5 % |
| Insurance | 1–2 % |
| **Profit before tax** | **15–25 %** |

- **Rule:** below a 15 % margin on sales, the risk is not worth it.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the option to buy registered, Nicolás got down to his CRP. Let me tell you how he built it.

**First, without IVA.** His whole account is without IVA, as the lesson says: the IVA he pays on the works he recovers when he charges 10 % on the sales. The ITP on the purchase, on the other hand, he does not recover, because a private individual is selling (Lesson 1.4): that is why it goes into the cost of the land. He went over it with his advisory firm, the one that handles the company.

**Then, the lines of his development.** He followed the lesson's structure from top to bottom:

- **Revenue:** his 18 homes, 2 of them penthouses.
- **Land:** the price of the plot, 71.392,05 €, with its ITP.
- **Costs:** construction, external works and utility connections, professional fees, licences, insurance, sales and marketing, overheads and finance costs, one line each.
- **At the very bottom:** the profit before tax and, after corporate income tax (Impuesto sobre Sociedades), the net profit.

**The tool.** So as not to leave out any item, he opened **Template 2 · Quick feasibility and residual method**, which comes with these lines (you have it in Lesson 2.3). He entered what he already knew: Comunitat Valenciana; plot without IVA, because a private individual is selling; 1.586,49 m² built and 18 homes. In each item he left the percentage the template comes with. He is still missing two figures: the sale price, which he works out in Lesson 2.2, and the construction cost, which he refines in Lesson 2.3.

**His yardstick.** He noted down the table of weights, to compare each item with once he has it: construction, between 45 and 55 % of revenue; profit before tax, between 15 and 25 %. And the rule: below a 15 % margin on sales, the risk is not worth it. He already knows the land weighs little: in the rough numbers it was 3,6 % of revenue (Lesson 1.1), well below the table's 15–30 %, because these are the prices of the professional's example.

### Summary

The CRP takes away from the revenue without IVA all the costs of the development, from the land to the finance costs, and leaves you the profit before tax. Compare each item with the indicative weights: below a 15 % margin on sales, it is not worth it.

## Lesson 2.2 — Fit and revenue: the market study

> **Where we are:** in Lesson 2.1 you set up the structure of your CRP. Now you fill it in from the top: the architect's fit study tells you which areas you are selling and the market study, at what price.

- **Fit study first.** Commission a reliable architecture practice to do the corresponding fit study for the development you plan to build. The most important things the architect does:
  - It estimates the buildability that counts and the built and usable areas.
  - It lays out the homes, common areas, stair and lift cores, garages and storerooms.
  - It checks unit types, daylight, ventilation and accessibility.
  - It tells you whether an additional planning or management instrument is needed.

- **Areas:**
  - Usable: what the buyer walks on.
  - Built: usable + walls (≈ usable × 1,15–1,20).
  - Built including common areas: + a share of entrance halls, stairs and lifts (≈ usable × 1,25–1,35). This is the one usually advertised.
  - Computable: the one that counts towards buildability under your PGOU.
- **How to calculate the sale price of the development:**
  1. **Detailed market study:** look at what is around you and who your competition is. Visit the developments on sale within a radius of 1-3 km and simulate a purchase (mystery shopping). Note the m² built and usable, the €/m², the unit type and the specification (memoria de calidades). Their websites usually show the plans and the specification; if they don't give prices, ask at the sales office or the developer.
  2. Read the quarterly reports of Tinsa, Sociedad de Tasación, Gloval and the consultancies (CBRE, JLL, Savills, Colliers).
  3. Apply a 3-5 % discount to the competition's asking prices for your first off-plan sales.
- **Indicative product mix:**

| Unit type | % | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 bedroom (50-65 m²) | 10–20 % | Highest €/m², investors |
| 2 bedrooms (70-85 m²) | 35–40 % | The most in demand |
| 3 bedrooms (90-110 m²) | 35–40 % | Families |
| 4 bedrooms (120 m²+) | 5–10 % | High ticket, slow turnover |
| Penthouses | depends on the building | 20-40 % premium |
| Garage | per space | 10.000–35.000 € depending on the city |
| Storeroom | per unit | 3.000–8.000 € |

- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** using advertised prices; designing the mix the developer likes rather than the one the area wants; forgetting that unsold garages stay on the balance sheet.

- **What you must also measure in the market:**
  - **Absorption:** homes sold per month per comparable development, and the total selling period.
  - **Future supply:** developments in the area with a licence granted or in process (ask at the town hall and look at the architects' association's statistics on endorsed designs).
  - **Buyer profile** and demand by unit type and size.
  - **Separate prices** for the home, the garage and the storeroom.
  - Always check advertised prices against real transactions.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

Nicolás's CRP was missing its first line, the revenue. Let me tell you how he worked it out.

**First, the fit study.** He went back to the architecture practice that did his massing study (Lesson 1.2), the same one that will draw up the design (Module 3), and commissioned the fit study for the development. The architect estimated the buildability that counts and the built and usable areas; laid out the homes, the common areas and the stair and lift core; checked unit types, daylight, ventilation and accessibility, and confirmed to him that no other planning instrument is needed. Result: 1.586,49 m² built in B+5, with no basement, with 18 homes, 2 of them penthouses.

**Then, the market study, on foot.** He looked at what was around him and who his competition was: he went round the developments on sale within 1-3 km of his plot and at each one he played the buyer, which is the mystery shopping technique. He noted the €/m², the unit type and the specification. He also read the quarterly reports of Tinsa, Sociedad de Tasación and Gloval.

**What is moving in the area.** He also measured the other things the lesson asks for: the absorption of the comparable developments, the future supply (he asked at the town hall's planning department and looked at the Association of Architects' statistics on endorsed designs) and the buyer profile.

**The mix, with the architect.** With what the area wants, and not with what he liked, he settled the mix: 2-bedroom homes, the most in demand, and 3-bedroom ones for families, with a mid-range specification, for first-home buyers.

**His price.** He applied the 3-5 % discount on the competition's asking prices that the lesson recommends for the first off-plan sales. It comes to **1.259,70 € per m² built, without IVA**: the same as the rough numbers required (Lesson 1.1). Over 18 homes, an average price of 111.027,86 € without IVA (122.130,64 € with 10 % IVA) and sales of 1.998.501,45 €. He entered it in Template 2.

### Summary

The architect gives you the fit study and the areas you sell; the market study, done on foot and looking at the competition, gives you the price. With both you settle the mix and the revenue in your CRP.

## Lesson 2.3 — Costs, precisely

> **Where we are:** you now have the revenue in your CRP (Lesson 2.2). Now the costs, item by item, and with them the residual method: how much you can pay for the land.

- **Land:** price + non-recoverable ITP/AJD + notary + registry + topographic survey + geotechnical study + lawyers + finance cost of the land + urbanisation charges.
- **Residual method:** `Maximum land price = Revenue − all other costs − target profit`. The land is worth what the project can afford to pay.
- **Construction (material execution cost, 2025-2026, above ground):**
  - Standard: 900–1.100 €/m² built
  - Upper-mid: 1.100–1.400 €/m²
  - Luxury: from 1.400 €/m²
  - Basement (below ground): 500–800 €/m²
  - Always ask local contractors for prices: they vary by province.
- **Budgets:**
  - **PEM** (material execution budget): the architect calculates it in the design.
  - **PEC** (contract budget) = PEM + overheads (13–17 %) + contractor's profit (6 %). That benchmark comes from public procurement (RD 1098/2001); on private works it is negotiated.
  - IVA on the works: 10 % (construction of homes contracted by the developer, art. 91.Uno.3.1.º LIVA). It is recoverable.
- **Other costs:**

| Item | Benchmark |
|---|---|
| Architect (design + works direction) | 3–5 % of the PEM (fees are free) |
| Technical architect (direction of works execution) | 1,5–3 % of the PEM |
| Health and safety coordination | 0,5–1 % of the PEM |
| Engineering (structure, building services) | 1–2 % of the PEM |
| Geotechnical study | 2.000–8.000 € |
| OCT | 0,3–0,6 % of the PEM |
| ICIO | up to 4 % of the actual cost of the works (check the by-law) |
| Licence fee | per by-law |
| Ten-year insurance | 0,8–1,5 % of the construction cost |
| TRC (contractors' all risks: damage to the works during construction) + RC (third-party liability: damage to third parties during the works) | 0,2–0,5 % |
| Guarantees or insurance for advance payments | 0,5–1,5 % a year on the amount guaranteed |
| Notary and registry for new building and horizontal division | per official scale |
| Sales and marketing | 2–4 % of sales |
| Finance costs | 3–6 % of revenue |
- **Who handles what** (whom you ask for the price of each item in the table above):
  - **Architect:** the design and the works direction, and the engineering (structure, building services), which the architect coordinates.
  - **Technical architect and health and safety coordinator** (Association of Technical Architects, Module 3): the direction of works execution and the health and safety coordination.
  - **Geotechnical laboratory:** the geotechnical study.
  - **OCT:** its review of the design (Module 3) and of the works (Module 7).
  - **Town hall:** the ICIO and the licence fee, in its tax by-law.
  - **Insurance broker:** the ten-year insurance, the TRC, the RC and the insurance for advance payments; the guarantees, the **bank** (Module 5).
  - **Notary and Land Registry:** the new building and the horizontal division, per official scale.
  - **Estate agency or sales agency:** the sales and marketing (Module 8).
  - **Bank:** the finance costs (Lesson 5.1).

- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** leaving out utility connections (electricity and water can cost tens of thousands of euros); forgetting the cost of guarantees; putting the ICIO on the PEM without reading the by-law.

- **Contingencies:** set aside 3–5 % of the construction cost from the very first CRP.
- **Legal costs** (lawyers, notary, registry) and **corporate structure** costs: include them even if they look small.
- To put all this in methodically, use **Template 2 · Quick feasibility and residual method** (included): sales, costs by item, margin and maximum land price. If you want the month-by-month detail (cost by chapter, loan and cash flow), the extra at the end of the course is the full professional model (**FINANCIALS** section).
- **A complete preliminary study, worked through:** the professional's **Feasibility analysis guide** (*Guía de análisis de viabilidad*: 22 homes, with its notes on the timetable) comes with the professional template, in the extra at the end of the course.

### Cost structure of a property development

The cost structure of a property development is divided into the following headings:

- Land development and consolidation costs.
- Plot acquisition costs.
- Professional fees of the technical team.
- Construction costs.
- Development costs.
- Sales and marketing costs.
- Financing, and costs in relation to sale prices and terms.
- Recommendations on sale prices and terms.

#### Development costs

The breakdown of development costs.

When you start a property project, you must take into account all the costs involved in creating it. Here the **management controller** is the person in charge of supervising and defining the management control policies, analysing results against the budgets, spotting deviations and proposing corrections.

They also supervise and define the management control policies, reviewing the budgets and identifying possible deviations.

In turn, they organise the proper flow between the financial data and the control system.

#### Professional fees of the technical team

Considering the preliminary study before building:

**Preliminary study.** This is the preliminary stage in which the ideas that develop the commission are expressed in a basic, schematic way, through sketches or drawings, to scale or not.

It includes gathering and organising the information needed, setting out the technical brief of requirements and an indicative estimate of the cost, so that the developer can take an initial decision. This stage will include the following documents:

- Explanatory report.
- Sketches or drawings, to scale or not.
- Cost estimate.

Fees in euros: `H = 0,05 · C · PEM · Ktotal`

Complexity coefficient: `Ktotal = 1 + ΣKparc`

**Outline design.** This is the stage of the work that sets out the fundamental aspects of the general characteristics of the building (functional, formal, constructional and economic), in order to give a first overall picture of it and establish a preliminary budget. This stage will include the following documents:

- Report justifying the general solutions adopted.
- Plans, elevations and sections to scale, without dimensions.
- Preliminary budget with an overall estimate, by built area or by another method considered suitable.

Fees in euros: `H = 0,05 · C · PEM · Ktotal`

Complexity coefficient: `Ktotal = 1 + ΣKparc`

**Basic design.** This is the stage of the work in which the general characteristics of the building are defined precisely, by adopting and justifying specific solutions.

Its content is enough to apply for the municipal licence or other administrative authorisations, once the compulsory endorsement by the professional body (*visado*) has been obtained, but not enough to carry out the construction. This stage will include at least the following documents:

- Report describing the general characteristics of the building and justifying the specific solutions that meet the administrative purpose referred to in the previous paragraph.
- General drawings to scale and dimensioned: plans, elevations and sections.
- Budget, with an overall estimate for each chapter, trade and technology.

These must be developed as specified in the CTE, where it applies.

Fees in euros: `H = 0,4 · C · PEM · Ktotal`

Complexity coefficient: `Ktotal = 1 + ΣKparc`

**Construction design.** The stage of the work in which the basic design is developed, with full details and specifications of all the materials, elements, construction systems and equipment, defining the building in its entirety.

Its content will be what is needed to carry out the works, with the compulsory endorsement by the professional body and the corresponding licence. This stage will include at least the following documents:

- Report on the foundations, structure and trades.
- Foundation and structure drawings.
- Detail drawings.
- Diagrams and sizing of the building services.
- Specifications: technical, general and particular conditions.
- Statement of measurements.
- Budgets obtained by applying unit prices for the work.

These must be developed as specified in the CTE, where it applies. In professional practice, the documents for the basic and construction designs may be submitted separately, or merged into one under the common name: basic and construction design.

Fees in euros: `H = 0,7 · C · PEM · Ktotal`

**Works direction.** Under article 12 of the Ley de Ordenación de la Edificación: the works director is the party who, as part of the site supervision team, directs the progress of the works in their technical, aesthetic, planning and environmental aspects, in accordance with the design that defines them, the building licence and the other compulsory authorisations, and the terms of the contract, in order to ensure that they are fit for the intended purpose.

#### Construction costs

When talking about construction costs, you need to take into account the **building reference costs**.

Basically, as the name suggests, they are values meant as a reference for general situations within a given area. The values obtained form a range between an indicative maximum and minimum for building.

Building reference costs come from a method that has no legal basis whatsoever, and which originated when the minimum reference costs that the respective professional associations (*Colegios Profesionales*) used to set disappeared; its sole purpose is to give users guidance on construction costs.

This method was developed by the Technical Standards, Supervision and Control Area (*Área de Normativa Técnica, Supervisión y Control*), and since then it has been included as a tool in the various editions of the construction database. It is a simple, explicit procedure made available to users as a first approximation to construction costs, or as a reference.

Some institutions, within their powers and responsibilities, have adopted it as it is or refined it to suit their specific needs.

Therefore, reference costs do not prevail over those set by the architect, and so they are not binding for calculating the planning fee and the tax on construction, installations and works (ICIO), which is managed by the town halls.

#### Sales and marketing costs

Within sales and marketing costs we can distinguish different types:

- **Advertising.** It covers the costs of advertising in any media and, for some property companies, the rental of the sales and customer service cabin.
- **Sales.** Some of the costs of marketing or selling the properties will be the sales staff, who are responsible for giving information about the products for sale, in this case the properties.
- **After-sales service.** This service is responsible for overseeing the management and control of the development's handover process, bearing in mind that the first months after the sale are when most complaints arise, and they need a quick and effective solution.

## Lesson 2.4 — Ratios and the decision

> **Where we are:** your CRP now has revenue (Lesson 2.2) and costs (Lesson 2.3). Now you work out the ratios, test three scenarios and decide on the numbers whether the deal goes ahead: GO / NO GO.

- **Margin on sales** = profit before tax ÷ revenue. Minimum 15 %.
- **ROE** = net profit ÷ equity. Target > 20 % a year.
- **Project and equity IRR (TIR):** on the monthly cash flow. Target 15–20 % for the project, 20 %+ on equity.
- **Typical duration:** 3-4 years from buying the land to the last deed.
- **Corporate income tax (IS, periods starting in 2026, Ley 7/2024):**
  - General: 25 %
  - Newly created entity: 15 % in the first period with a positive tax base and the next one
  - Small company (turnover < 10 M€): 23 %
  - Micro-company (< 1 M€): 19 % on the first 50.000 € and 21 % on the rest
  - Navarra and País Vasco: their own rules
  - Confirm it with your adviser: there are exclusions (asset-holding entities), and a development's turnover varies a lot from one year to the next.
- **Sensitivity scenarios:**

| Scenario | Assumptions |
|---|---|
| Optimistic | Sales at 100 % of price, construction on budget |
| Base | Sales at 95 %, construction +5 % |
| Pessimistic | Sales at 85–90 %, construction +10–15 %, 6 months' delay |

- **Decision:** GO only if the pessimistic case still makes no loss.
- ⚠️ **Typical mistakes:** calculating the margin with IVA included; leaving out the calendar (IRR depends on time); forgetting IS in the ROE.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With Template 2 complete, it was time for Nicolás to decide. Let me tell you how he did it.

**The margin on sales.** His CRP, without IVA: sales, 1.998.501,45 €; total cost of the development, 1.522.299,21 €; profit before tax, 476.202,24 €. The margin is the profit divided by the revenue: 476.202,24 € ÷ 1.998.501,45 € = 24 %. It clears the 15 % minimum comfortably.

**The land.** He pays 45 € of land per m² built. The residual method lets him pay up to 178.247,08 € for the plot; the owner is asking 71.392,05 €, 106.855,03 € below the maximum.

**The three scenarios.** The optimistic one is his CRP as it stands: sales at 100 % of the price and construction with no overruns. For the other two he went back to Template 2 and moved the sale price and the construction cost as the table says:

- **Base** (sales at 95 %, construction +5 %): profit of 324.539,59 €, a 17 % margin.
- **Pessimistic** (sales at 90 %, construction +10 %): profit of 172.876,94 €, 9,6 %. The template does not calculate the 6 months' delay: with the loan the bank is offering him, it means 23.976,21 € more in interest (4,5 % a year on 1.065.609,45 €, for half a year). He still makes no loss.
- **The table's extreme** (sales at 85 %, construction +15 % and the delay): the profit falls to 21.214,28 € before that interest, so he ends up breaking even. He will keep an eye on it with the exit milestones in his investment memorandum (Lesson 2.5).

**The calendar.** The IRR (TIR) depends on time, and Template 2 does not go month by month. With his advisory firm he built the monthly cash flow: when he pays for the plot, the designs and each construction valuation, and when he collects the sales, with 20 months of works and 4 more until the sale deeds. From it he worked out the project IRR and the equity IRR. The professional template in the extra has it month by month (Lesson 2.3).

**The equity.** He had with his bank the preliminary conversation in the "Where to go" table: indicative terms and what it will ask of him in pre-sales. With a loan of 70 % of the cost, his equity is 30 %: 456.689,76 €, and the land already paid for counts. He will close it with Template 3 (Lesson 5.1). You have the banks in your province at the end of the module.

**The tax, for the ROE.** The ROE is on the net profit, after corporate income tax. His SPE is new: on the lesson's list it would be entitled to the 15 % for a newly created entity, and he had his advisory firm confirm it.

**GO.** A 24 % margin, land well below the maximum and a pessimistic case that still makes a profit: Nicolás decides to go ahead with the development, knowing that at the table's worst extreme he breaks even. Next, buying (Lesson 2.5).

### Summary

The margin on sales (minimum 15 %), the ROE and the IRR (TIR) tell you whether the deal is worth it: always without IVA and with the calendar built in. You test three scenarios and give the GO only if the pessimistic case still makes no loss.

## Lesson 2.5 — Negotiation, structure and approval of the investment

> **Where we are:** with the GO from Lesson 2.4, you close the deal: you negotiate with the owner, put the company and the partners in order, approve the investment and buy the plot.

- **Negotiating with the plot's owner:**
  - Price and payment terms: cash, deferred with an option to buy, or part of the price in a property swap.
  - **Option to buy** and enough time to do the studies and get financing: formalise it with a sale contract with a purchase option.
    - It can be **free** or in exchange for an **agreed amount** (a percentage of the price, for example 3 %). When little is selling, the owner usually grants it free; when the market is good, he asks for that amount, which you lose if in the end you don't buy: it compensates him for keeping the plot tied up for you.
    - During that time you finish the studies, get the financing and prepare the project. If it works out, you exercise the option and buy; if not, you don't buy and the owner keeps his plot.
  - **Conditions precedent:** planning, registry, technical and financial feasibility; obtaining the licence.
  - Formulas: direct sale, option to buy, **swap** for future homes, or **contribution of the land** to the company in exchange for shares.
- **Legal and corporate structure:**
  - **New SPV or existing company.** The SPV ring-fences the risk and makes it easier for partners to come in and go out; the existing company brings its banking track record.
  - **Partners and contributions:** capital or shareholder loan. A participating loan (RDL 7/1996, art. 20) counts as equity for company-law purposes.
  - **Shareholders' agreement:** reinforced majorities, division of roles, additional contributions if there are overruns, deadlock, exit (drag-along and tag-along), distribution of profits.
  - Notarial **powers of attorney** for whoever manages, and the **guarantees** the banks ask for (personal guarantees).
  - Review the **tax** treatment of the purchase and of the development before signing.
- **Approving the investment:**
  - Prepare an **investment memorandum**: plot, product, CRP, cash flow, sensitivities, risks, calendar and financing.
  - The investment committee or the **shareholders' meeting** approves it. In a capital company, buying an asset worth more than 25 % of the total assets on the balance sheet is a matter for the general meeting (art. 160.f LSC).
  - Set the approved **budget, minimum return, calendar and financing**.
  - Set **control milestones and exit or renegotiation criteria**. Examples: licence not granted within X months; pre-sales below 30 % within Y months; construction tender more than 8 % over budget.
- **Acquiring the plot:**
  1. Final checks on signing day: that day's *nota simple*, IBI and debt certificates.
  2. Public deed of sale.
  3. Tax returns: ITP/AJD on your community's form 600, within its deadline (usually 30 working days); IVA charged by the seller if the sale is subject to IVA.
  4. Registration at the Land Registry.
  5. Cancellation or regularisation of the agreed encumbrances, and actual taking of possession.
- **Who handles what** (whom you ask for each item in the lists above):
  - **You:** the negotiation with the owner (price and payment terms) and the investment memorandum.
  - **Lawyer:** reviews the sale contract with a purchase option and the conditions precedent, and drafts the shareholders' agreement.
  - **Advisory firm or tax adviser:** the partners' contributions (capital or shareholder loan) and the tax treatment of the purchase and of the development.
  - **Notary:** the notarial powers of attorney and the public deed of sale.
  - **Shareholders' meeting** (or investment committee): the approval of the investment.
  - **Bank:** the guarantees it asks you for, such as personal guarantees.
  - **Land Registry:** that day's *nota simple* and the registration.
  - **Town hall · Tax office:** the IBI and debt certificates.
  - **Your community's tax authority:** the ITP/AJD on form 600, within its deadline.
  - **Seller:** the IVA it charges, if the sale is subject to IVA; the cancellation or regularisation of the agreed encumbrances and handing over possession.

### Example: Nicolás, the developer

With the GO, Nicolás closed the deal. Let me tell you in what order.

**What he negotiated with the owner.** He had it tied up in the option to buy from Lesson 1.4: the price, 71.392,05 €; the payment terms, deferred with an option to buy, and enough time to do the studies and get financing, which is what the lesson asks for. In writing, the conditions precedent of planning, registry and technical feasibility: if any of them failed, he would not buy.

**The structure.** His SPE already exists (Lesson 1.4): a new company just for this development, what the lesson calls an SPV, which ring-fences the risk. With his advisory firm he decided how the equity goes in (capital or shareholder loan) and reviewed the tax treatment before signing: the purchase pays ITP, because a private individual is selling, and the sales will carry 10 % IVA. He signed his SPE's **shareholders' agreement** with what the lesson asks for: reinforced majorities, division of roles, additional contributions if there are overruns, deadlock, exit and distribution of profits. He knows the bank will ask him for personal guarantees (Lesson 5.1).

**The investment memorandum.** It covered the plot, his product, the CRP, the cash flow, the three scenarios, the risks, the calendar and the financing. He set a budget of 1.522.299,21 €, a minimum return of a 15 % margin on sales, his calendar and his financing. And the control milestones and exit criteria, with the lesson's examples and a deadline for each: licence not granted, pre-sales below 30 % and construction tender more than 8 % over budget. It was approved by his SPE's **shareholders' meeting**.

**The purchase.** With the investment approved, he exercised his option:

1. On signing day, **that day's _nota simple_** from the **Land Registry**, and the IBI and debt certificates from the **town hall's tax office**.
2. The **public deed of sale**, at the same notary's as the option.
3. The Comunitat Valenciana's **ITP**, 9 %: 6.425,28 €, on form 600 and within its deadline (usually 30 working days).
4. **Registration** at the Land Registry, in his SPE's name.
5. With no encumbrances to cancel (Lesson 1.3), taking possession of the plot.

The plot now belongs to his SPE, conveyed by deed and registered. He moves on to Module 3: the technical designs.
